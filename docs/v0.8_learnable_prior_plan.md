# MAGI v0.8 — learnable prior & flow conditioning, explained

Audience: this is written to be readable **without** a background in
normalizing flows or variational inference. It explains *why* we need a
learnable prior, *what* the pieces are, and *how* they map onto the actual
MAGI code, before giving the concrete implementation steps. It builds on the
terser, expert-level `docs/v0.8_IAF_integration_plan.md` (read this one
first) and continues from `docs/v0.8_fixing_plan.md` §8 (the flow-continuum
work already done).

---

## 1. The problem, in one paragraph

We gave the energy head a normalizing flow so it can represent sharp,
peaked, oddly-shaped continua (it can — that part works: the flow reproduced
CryoSphere-Small's sharp peak on the synthetic test). But when we let the
flow condition on the latent `z` — which we **must**, because that is how
energy stays correlated with geometry — the *generated* energy spectrum came
out wrong and got **worse** the longer we trained, even though the training
loss kept improving. That paradox (training better, generation worse) is a
classic, well-understood VAE failure called the **aggregated-posterior /
prior mismatch**. The fix is a **learnable prior**. The rest of this document
explains what that sentence means and how to build it.

---

## 2. A refresher on how the CVAE generates

MAGI is a Conditional Variational Autoencoder. Three moving parts:

- **Encoder** `q(z | x, cond)`: takes a real event `x` (energy + geometry)
  and its particle type `cond`, and outputs a small latent vector `z` (8
  numbers). In code it outputs `z_mean`, `z_logvar` and we sample
  `z = z_mean + exp(½·z_logvar)·ε` (`model.py::sample_z`). Think of `z` as a
  compressed "recipe" for that event.
- **Decoder** `p(x | z, cond)`: takes a recipe `z` and a particle type, and
  reconstructs the event — energy via the mixture/flow head, geometry via the
  Gaussian heads (`model.py::_decode_params`).
- **Prior** `p(z)`: the distribution we *assume* the recipes follow. Today it
  is fixed to a standard Gaussian `N(0, I)` — 8 independent unit normals.

**Training** pushes two goals at once (the "ELBO"): reconstruct `x` well, and
keep each event's encoded `z` close to the prior (the "KL" term). Because both
the encoder's `q(z|x)` and the prior `N(0,I)` are Gaussian, the KL has a
tidy closed-form formula — that is the `kl_per = -0.5 * sum(1 + z_logvar -
z_mean² - exp(z_logvar))` line you see in `train_step`.

**Generation** (`model.py::generate`) does the reverse and is the key point:
we do **not** have a real event to encode, so we invent recipes by sampling
`z ~ N(0, I)` (the prior) and decode them. **Generation quality depends
entirely on the prior matching the recipes the decoder was actually trained
on.**

---

## 3. The precise bug: the prior doesn't match the recipes

During training the decoder only ever sees recipes produced by the encoder —
call the collection of all of them the **aggregated posterior**
`q(z) = average over training events of q(z | x)`. The decoder becomes an
expert at decoding *those specific* recipes.

At generation we feed it recipes drawn from `N(0, I)` instead. If the
aggregated posterior `q(z)` is not actually shaped like `N(0, I)` — and it
never is exactly — then we are handing the decoder recipes from regions it
rarely or never trained on ("prior holes"), and it produces garbage or
badly-weighted output there.

**Why it hits the energy flow so hard, but not the geometry heads.** The KL
term nudges `q(z)` toward `N(0,I)` but never makes it exact. The leftover gap
is small, and a *low-capacity* head (the geometry Gaussians) can only respond
to `z` mildly, so the gap barely shows. The energy **flow** is
*high-capacity*: it can carve very different energy densities for slightly
different `z`. So it eagerly exploits `z` to drive the training loss down —
effectively memorizing "this recipe's event sat at this energy" — and that is
exactly the behavior that falls apart when generation samples `z` from the
mismatched prior. This is why, in our runs, the z-conditioned flow was great
early (before it had exploited `z`) and progressively worse later.

We confirmed this precisely (see `v0.8_fixing_plan.md` §8): with the flow
conditioned on `z`, generation degraded over training; forcing it to
condition only on `cond` (removing `z`) fixed generation but threw away the
energy↔geometry coupling we need.

---

## 4. The fix, intuitively: stop assuming, start learning the prior

Instead of *assuming* the recipes follow `N(0,I)` and sampling from that,
**learn the actual distribution of recipes** and sample from *that* at
generation. That learned distribution is the **learnable prior**
`p(z | cond)`.

Analogy: the decoder is a chef who only knows how to cook from the recipes
it was trained on (the aggregated posterior). Today at "generation" we hand
it random recipes from a generic cookbook (`N(0,I)`) — many are nonsense to
this chef. A learnable prior is a cookbook we *fit* to the chef's actual
training recipes, so every recipe we hand over is one it knows how to cook.

Crucially, this **keeps** the energy↔geometry coupling: the decoder still
reads `z`, `z` still carries the shared structure that ties energy to the
geometry outputs, we have just made sure the `z` we sample at generation is
realistic. It fixes generation for **every** head at once, not just energy.

## 5. Normalizing flows, from scratch (the tool we build the prior with)

We can't write down the shape of `q(z)` by hand — it's some complicated
8-dimensional blob. A **normalizing flow** is a way to *learn* an arbitrary
distribution by starting from a simple one and warping it.

- Start with a simple base variable `u ~ N(0, I)` (easy to sample).
- Apply an **invertible** function `z = f(u)` (a neural network built to be
  reversible). Warping a simple distribution through `f` can produce an
  arbitrarily complicated distribution over `z`.
- Because `f` is invertible, we can also go backward `u = f⁻¹(z)`, and the
  **change-of-variables formula** gives the exact density:
  `log p(z) = log p_base(f⁻¹(z)) + log |det ∂f⁻¹/∂z|`.
  (The second term accounts for how `f` stretches/squeezes space — the same
  kind of Jacobian term already in our continuum flow's `log_prob`.)

So a flow gives us two operations:
- **sample**: draw `u ~ N(0,I)`, push forward `z = f(u)`. (used at generation)
- **log_prob(z)**: run backward and apply the formula. (used at training)

We already use exactly this machinery in `core/flows.py` for the 1-D energy
continuum. The prior is the same idea in 8-D.

### 5a. IAF vs MAF vs coupling — and why the direction matters

Flows differ in how cheap each direction (forward-sample vs backward-score)
is. This is the single most important design point, so:

- **MAF** (Masked Autoregressive Flow): cheap to evaluate `log_prob` of a
  given point, **expensive to sample** (must generate one dimension at a time,
  8 sequential passes).
- **IAF** (Inverse Autoregressive Flow) = MAF run in reverse: cheap to
  **sample** in one pass, expensive to score an arbitrary external point.
- **Coupling flows** (RealNVP / Glow / neural-spline coupling): cheap in
  **both** directions. Slightly less expressive per layer than autoregressive,
  but you stack a few layers and it's plenty.

Now match those to our two possible uses:

| Where | What it needs | Best choice |
|---|---|---|
| **Posterior** `q(z|x)` | richer encoder, scored only at self-sampled points | **IAF** (cheap sample; only ever scores its own draws) — this is where the "IAF" name comes from |
| **Prior** `p(z\|cond)` | must be **sampled** at generation AND **scored** at training | **coupling** (needs both directions cheap) — NOT IAF |

**The load-bearing fact for us:** the thing that fixes *generation* is the
**prior**, and the prior must be a **coupling flow** (cheap to sample), not an
IAF. A posterior IAF is a separate, optional upgrade that improves the ELBO /
representation but does **not**, by itself, change generation (generation
never runs the posterior). So despite "IAF" being in the title of the older
plan, **the priority piece is a conditional coupling-flow prior.**

## 6. Concrete implementation against the MAGI code

All in `CVAE_MixEnergy_ContPhi_TaskAdaptive` (`core/model.py`) plus a small
new module. Everything is additive and flag-gated so `prior="gaussian"`
(default) reproduces today's behavior byte-for-byte, exactly like the
`continuum_mode`/`energy_flow_condition` switches already added.

**(a) New module `core/priors.py`** — `ConditionalCouplingPrior`, a small
stack of affine/spline **coupling** layers over the 8-D `z`, conditioned on
`cond`. Two methods, mirroring `core/flows.py`:
- `log_prob(z, cond) -> (batch,)` — used in the KL.
- `sample(n, cond) -> (n, latent_dim)` — used in `generate()`.
Reuse the coupling/scale-clamp patterns already proven in
`NormalFlow_Fioretti/increaseSPO_L2_NF.py` (port from torch to TF), or tfp
`RealNVP`/`RationalQuadraticSpline` coupling bijectors (tfp is now a
dependency and verified working on this stack).

**(b) KL becomes a Monte-Carlo estimate** (`train_step` / `test_step`). Today
the KL is closed-form because prior = `N(0,I)`. With a learned prior it isn't,
so we estimate it from the `z` we already sampled:
```
# today (keep for prior="gaussian"):
kl_per = -0.5 * sum(1 + z_logvar - z_mean² - exp(z_logvar))

# learned prior (prior="coupling"):
log_qz = gaussian_log_prob(z; z_mean, z_logvar)      # encoder density at z
log_pz = self.prior.log_prob(z, cond)                # learned prior density at z
kl_per = log_qz - log_pz
```
Nothing else in the loss (reconstruction, TaskAdaptive weights, the energy
flow, sigma regularizers) changes.

**(c) Generation samples the learned prior** (`generate()`), one line:
```
# z = tf.random.normal((n_samples, latent_dim))     # today
z = self.prior.sample(n_samples, cond)               # learned prior
```
This is the line that actually fixes faithful generation. The energy flow and
gate keep conditioning on `z` (via `energy_cont_feat` / `energy_feat`), so the
energy↔geometry coupling is fully retained — we have only made `z` realistic.

**(d) Optional, later — posterior IAF** (`core/flows.py` can host it, sketch
already in `v0.8_IAF_integration_plan.md` §3). Improves the ELBO and
reconstructions; independent of the prior; ship only if the prior alone
doesn't close the gap. Not needed for the first faithful-generation result.

**(e) Checkpointing / flags** (`training/checkpointing.py`). Persist a
`"prior"` block in the metadata (type, n_coupling_layers, hidden sizes,
whether it conditions on cond) and assert it matches at generation-load time
— the same string-key drift guard the mixture-energy metadata already needs.
Constructor kwarg `prior="gaussian"|"coupling"` (default gaussian).

## 7. How we'll know it works (validation)

1. **First, a synthetic test WITH real energy↔geometry correlation.** Our
   current single-peak test uses *noise* geometry, so a z-conditioned energy
   head can only ever leak there — that test **cannot** tell a good z-coupled
   model from a bad one. New test: inject a known dependence (e.g. energy
   drawn conditionally on a geometry variable), then check that generation
   reproduces both the energy marginal AND the energy-vs-geometry correlation.
   This is what actually gates the prior work; it's cheap and must come first.
2. **A/B via the existing Wasserstein diagnostics** (`magi.validation`) on the
   same preprocessed data: `prior="gaussian"` vs `prior="coupling"`, on both
   the energy marginal and the geometry distributions. Expect the coupling
   prior to fix the generated energy spectrum without hurting geometry.
3. **Then** the CR-multimodal synthetic sanity check and finally the real
   CR+Small run (with the line width pinned to the detector resolution you'll
   provide).
