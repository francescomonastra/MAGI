\section{Scope}

MAGI is a conditional variational autoencoder that replaces Geant4 transport
from a spacecraft or laboratory mass model down to a closed surface (the
``CryoSphere'') surrounding a cryogenic detector. Instead of tracking primaries
through the full assembly, MAGI generates particle crossings directly on that
surface; those crossings are then injected into the same mass model and
transported the short remaining distance.

The validation question is therefore narrow and well posed: \emph{does a MAGI
crossing produce detector events at the same rate and with the same spectrum as
a real recorded crossing, in the same geometry?}

Three independent sources have been tested: cosmic rays on the SRON X-IFU model
(CR), cosmic rays on the DM1.2 laboratory cryostat, and $^{40}$K
radioactivity on the SRON model.

\subsection{Model version under validation}

All results below are \textbf{v0.8.2}, class
\texttt{CVAE\_MixEnergy\_ContPhi\_TaskAdaptive}: conditional rational-quadratic
spline normalizing-flow continuum with CDF pre-warp, pinned 4\,eV emission
lines, focal-gated line/continuum supervision, and a learnable conditional
coupling prior with zone conditioning. \texttt{latent\_dim}\,=\,8.

v0.8.3 (\texttt{energy\_condition\_geometry=True}, \texttt{latent\_dim}\,=\,12)
was implemented and trained at three seeds and is \textbf{not adopted}
(\S\ref{sec:v083}).

\section{The normalization-free comparison}

The central method, used for every headline number here:

\begin{equation}
R \;=\;
\frac{N^{\mathrm{MAGI}}_{\mathrm{events}} / N^{\mathrm{MAGI}}_{\mathrm{crossings}}}
     {N^{\mathrm{real}}_{\mathrm{events}} / N^{\mathrm{real}}_{\mathrm{crossings}}}
\end{equation}

Both sides are counted \emph{per injected crossing}, through the identical mass
model, binary, physics list and scoring code. $R$ requires no exposure, no
detector area, no flux normalization, and---critically---no crossing efficiency
$\chi$. It is insensitive to every quantity that has historically caused
discordance between MAGI-side and Geant4-side bookkeeping.

The \emph{efficiency factor} quoted in some tables is a different normalization,
retained for continuity with earlier analysis:
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon \;=\;
\frac{N^{\mathrm{MAGI}}_{\mathrm{counts}} / N^{\mathrm{MAGI}}_{\mathrm{shot}}}
     {N^{\mathrm{ref}}_{\mathrm{counts}} / N^{\mathrm{ref}}_{\mathrm{shot}}},
\qquad
R = \varepsilon \times \chi ,
\end{equation}
where MAGI's shot particles are CryoSphere crossings and the reference's are
thrown primaries. A perfect surrogate returns $\varepsilon = 1/\chi$, not 1.

\paragraph{What can and cannot be claimed.}
\textbf{DM127 is an anti-coincidence detector} (\texttt{ACDSD} in
\texttt{GDMLDetectorConstruction.cc}), so the X-IFU 0.1--30\,keV science band is
\emph{not} the relevant band for it and must not be labelled as such --- the
0.1--30\,keV row is retained only because it is the band the SRON efficiency
table uses. The physically relevant band here is the MIP peak, and that is also
where the statistics are: \num{1325} reference events, $\pm2.7\,\%$, with
$\varepsilon$ within $0.4\,\%$ of ideal. The 0.1--30\,keV row is an
\emph{integrated rate only} --- 41 reference events, $\pm15.6\,\%$ before MAGI
contributes any error. More MAGI generation cannot improve it; the reference is
the cap.

DM1.2 has a \textbf{single} sensitive detector, so there is no across-detector
spread to quote as a systematic; the $\pm38.2$ is pure Poisson.

\paragraph{Structural checks (all pass).}
Crossing coverage \num{1733}/\num{1733} $= 100.0\,\%$ \ev{E2}; global species
fractions correct to $0.3\,\%$ ($\gamma$ 1.003, $e^{-}$ 1.002, $e^{+}$ 1.039,
$\mu^{-}$ 0.997) \ev{E2}; impact-parameter distribution 0.912--1.010 over
$b<1$ to $b<50$\,mm \ev{E2}.

\paragraph{Known defect: a coherent 4\,\% deposit shift.}
A two-sample Kolmogorov--Smirnov test on all deposits gives $D = 0.0558$,
$p = 0.0057$ \ev{E2}. This is \emph{not} a shape distortion but a uniform
downward shift: deposit quantiles 25--95\,\% run 0.984, 0.960, 0.959, 0.940,
0.962, and the maximum CDF separation falls on the MIP peak at 186\,keV. It
localises to the MIP band ($D=0.0594$, $p=0.012$); the 300--1000\,keV band and
everything below 100\,keV are indistinguishable ($p = 0.81$ and $0.99$).

The cause is path length. Among detector-aimed crossings MAGI's tracks are too
close to normal incidence: median $\sec\theta$ is 0.955 of the reference value,
against a measured deposit-median ratio of 0.960 \ev{E4}. Underlying it is a
\emph{direction-conditional species-mix error}: at $b<10$\,mm MAGI runs
$+8.8\,\%$ muons, $-13.6\,\%$ $e^{-}$, $-7.7\,\%$ $\gamma$, while global
fractions are correct to $0.3\,\%$ \ev{E4}. Per-species energies are correct
(muons 0.99--1.03 at every cut).

\paragraph{Prerequisite finding: a geometry overlap that destroyed 39\,\% of crossings.}
At $R=105$\,mm the CryoSphere intersected \texttt{PlateCu1} (a 150\,mm-radius,
5\,mm-thick copper disc at $z=106.5$, spanning $z\in[104,109]$) by at least
1.29\,mm --- 13$\times$ the shell thickness --- and was placed with overlap
checking disabled. Geant4 navigation inside an overlap is undefined, and
$39.4\,\%$ of detector events had \emph{no recorded crossing}, uniformly
58--63\,\% across five independent runs, $93.5\,\%$ of them primary-muon
deposits \ev{E5}. Moving the sphere to $R=95$\,mm restores $100.0\,\%$
coverage. The SRON sphere was checked and is clean.

Separately, the DM1.2 muon macro had used \texttt{/gps/ang/type focused} ten
times, aiming every muon at the origin (median $\cos$ 0.994 to the origin,
$56.8\,\%$ inside $\cos>0.99$); absolute rates from that configuration were
never physical \ev{E5}. All DM1.2 results here are from the \texttt{iso}
rerun.

\paragraph{Identified contribution to the SRON deficit.}
An \textbf{inconsistent ingoing-particle cut} \ev{E8}. \texttt{EventAction.cc}

that code: the CR training set contains $10.37\,\%$ outgoing crossings and the
$^{40}$K set $22.53\,\%$, while the DM1.2 set contains $0.00\,\%$. MAGI applies
no ingoing/outgoing selection either, and generates $13.59\,\%$ outgoing against
the training set's $10.37\,\%$ --- accounting for $3.6\,\%$ of the rate deficit.
This also means \textbf{SRON and DM1.2 models are on different conventions.}

\section{Result 3 --- $^{40}$K, SRON BuildingModel}
\label{sec:k40}

\begin{callout}{warn}{red!4}{Correction 17/08/2026: the training population is
albedo, not the crossing population}
The $^{40}$K training set was recorded by
\texttt{Old-Simulations/Sim/S1GDMLSRON\_CryoSphereEmission},
whose \texttt{EventAction.cc:237} carries
\texttt{SphereCenter(0., 0., -3007.6*mm)} --- the 2500\,mm error of
\S\ref{sec:cr}. \textbf{$100.00\,\%$} of its crossings are ingoing with respect
to that wrong centre (and $97.28\,\%$ move downward), against $77.45\,\%$
ingoing w.r.t.\ the true $-507.66$. $^{40}$K gammas travel \emph{up} from the
floor, so a cut toward a centre 2500\,mm \emph{below} kept only photons that
passed the sphere, scattered off the structure above and came back.

The spectrum confirms it independently: the median is \textbf{211.2\,keV}
against the Compton $180^\circ$ backscatter energy of the 1460.8\,keV line,
\textbf{217.5\,keV}; $30.4\,\%$ of crossings lie within $\pm15\,\%$ of it, the
histogram peaks at 160--180\,keV, and the 1461\,keV line appears \emph{once in
1.87\,M} --- an unscattered line photon cannot be moving downward at the sphere.

Geometry was excluded by measurement, not assumed: an A/B gave 365.6\,keV
(original slab), 374.7\,keV (reduced slab) and 366.8\,keV (shielded
BuildingModel), all $\sim$370\,keV and insensitive to the slab at the $3\,\%$
level. Re-crossings ($0.86\,\%$ of events; first-crossing-only median unchanged
at 211.2\,keV) and species composition ($99.3\,\%$ gamma both sides) were
excluded too.

\textbf{Ra-226 and Th-232 are affected identically} and cannot substitute:
both are $100.00\,\%$ ingoing w.r.t.\ the buggy centre, both have medians at
$0.83$--$0.86$ of their backscatter energies, Ra-226 has \emph{zero} events at
its 1764.5\,keV line, and Th-232's maximum recorded energy (2505\,keV) falls
\emph{below} its 2614.5\,keV line altogether.

\textbf{What survives.} The numbers below are unchanged and remain a valid
\emph{closure} result --- MAGI reproduces the distribution it was trained on,
and that survives transport to detector level. They are \textbf{not} evidence
about $^{40}$K background physics, because both arms of the comparison draw on
the same biased population. Restated as a physics claim they would be wrong.
\end{callout}

\paragraph{$R = \mathbf{1.118 \pm 0.373}$, KS $D = 0.2384$, $p = 0.60$}
\ev{E9}: $2\times10^{8}$ injected on both sides into
\texttt{SRON\_CCNCoolerwithXFDM\_noShields\_BuildingModel.gdml}, giving 19 MAGI
counts (\num{9.50e-8} per crossing) against 17 from real recorded crossings
(\num{8.50e-8}). Matched $N$ by construction. \textbf{$^{40}$K passes at
detector level.}

The real-crossing arm recycles \num{1873609} recorded crossings $\sim$107$\times$,
which is sound because Geant4 re-randomizes the interaction on each replay;
continuity was verified from the crossing counts ($\sim$40.15\,M re-registered
per job, 0.80 per injected event, uniform across four jobs) rather than assumed
\ev{E9}.

\paragraph{The 22$\times$ gap --- superseded explanation.}
Against the 44-days reference the same run reads $0.049 \pm 0.012$. This was
previously attributed \ev{E10} to the two runs not sharing a $^{40}$K source
volume (a $57\times$ ratio between a $2000\times2000\times400$\,mm box at
$z=-1600$\,mm and $750\times750\times50$\,mm at $z=-1250$\,mm).

\textbf{That attribution is now known to be incomplete, and its direction was
wrong.} The user confirms the slab was the \emph{reduced} one and the model
unshielded; the old campaign's own macro samples the large box confined to the
reduced slab, so both runs sample the same physical volume and the $57\times$
never applied as stated. The dominant effect is the albedo selection above: the
training set retains only the back-scattered fraction, which both suppresses the
yield and softens the spectrum. The two still cannot be chained, and the
conclusion --- MAGI sits with the population it was trained on --- stands, but
for a different and larger reason. \textbf{$^{40}$K returns in September}
(\S\ref{sec:open}); this section is not to be used as a physics result before then.

\paragraph{Bookkeeping correction.}
\texttt{NgenPotassiumForMAGI\_v082} was declared \num{580000000}; measured from
the run's own output it is \textbf{\num{538205667}} \ev{E11}. The output file
concatenates two jobs, with exactly one backward \texttt{EventId} jump at row
\num{398400}; estimating each job separately by $N = \max\cdot(n+1)/n$ gives
\num{438145967} and \num{100059700}. The declared value is $7.8\,\%$ high and
outside the $95\,\%$ region of both jobs' order statistics.

The same estimator was run as a control on two other declared counts and
\emph{confirms} them ($^{40}$K v0.7.2 ratio 1.0001; CR v0.8.2 ratio 0.9998),
so it is not systematically low. Every v0.8.2 $^{40}$K \emph{Flower} flux and
efficiency rises by 1.0777; the Detector-1 efficiency goes $3472 \to 3741$.
The detector-level result above is unaffected --- it never uses the constant.

Both columns are meaningful and neither is wrong: per crossing measures how
efficiently a MAGI crossing converts into a detector event, per primary is the
flux. Quote the one the claim is about.
\end{callout}

\subsection{Localising the deficit: a classifier two-sample test}
\label{sec:classifier}

\paragraph{Real and generated crossings are separable.} A gradient-boosted
classifier trained to tell real from MAGI crossings, on the seven continuous
features plus species, reaches \textbf{AUC $= 0.68$} (0.5 would be
indistinguishable). This is a sharper instrument than anything used before: it
sees the joint, it is cheap, and on \num{4e5} samples per class its statistical
error is negligible next to the $\pm0.09$ on $R$.

\paragraph{The mismatch is diffuse, not a broken head.} Permutation importance is
flat across all six continuous variables and essentially zero on species:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrlr}
\toprule
feature & $\Delta$AUC & feature & $\Delta$AUC \\
\midrule
$\sin\phi_v$ & $+0.0724$ & $\log_{10}E$ & $+0.0556$ \\
$\sin\phi_r$ & $+0.0702$ & $u_r$        & $+0.0508$ \\
$u_v$        & $+0.0633$ & is\_gamma    & $+0.0049$ \\
$\cos\phi_v$ & $+0.0624$ & is\_$\mu^-$  & $+0.0008$ \\
$\cos\phi_r$ & $+0.0622$ & is\_$e^-$    & $+0.0003$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

A wrong marginal would put one variable far above the rest. Instead the
classifier separates only by reading the \emph{combination}. Per-species AUCs
agree ($\gamma$ 0.651, $\mu^-$ 0.614, $e^-$ 0.630), so it is not one population.
This is consistent with everything else measured: marginals reproduced to
5--11\,\% RMS, correlation residual $0.0147$ against KDSource's $0.0025$, and
DM1.2 over-dispersed at $1.536$ in nearest-neighbour distance
(\S\ref{sec:memorisation}).

\paragraph{Reweighting cannot repair it --- and that is the result.} The planned
test was to reweight MAGI to the real joint by likelihood ratio and re-inject. It
was run twice and \emph{refused to inject both times}, because the reweighted
sample stayed separable:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
 & AUC before & AUC after \\
\midrule
single-shot, uncalibrated, 2:1 pool & 0.672 & 0.608 \\
isotonic-calibrated, 4 iterations, 6:1 pool & 0.682 & 0.577 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The second attempt reached in-loop AUC $0.537$ but validated at $0.577$, with
maximum weight climbing $11 \to 52$ and effective sample size falling to
$5.4$\,M of 12\,M. That is weight degeneracy: \textbf{you cannot upweight samples
the generator never produced}. The obstruction is coverage, not density shape.

\paragraph{Where MAGI does not go.} With $c(x) = P(\text{real}\,|\,x)$:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
 & \% of real & \% of MAGI & density ratio \\
\midrule
$c>0.70$ & $7.62\,\%$ & $1.43\,\%$ & $5.3\times$ \\
$c>0.80$ & $2.73\,\%$ & $0.28\,\%$ & $9.9\times$ \\
$c>0.90$ & $0.62\,\%$ & $0.024\,\%$ & $25.8\times$ \\
$c>0.95$ & $0.10\,\%$ & $0.001\,\%$ & $\mathbf{104\times}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The under-covered events ($c>0.8$) are $87.4\,\%$ gamma against $56.9\,\%$
overall, with median energy \textbf{511.0\,keV} against 2831\,keV, median
$u_r = +0.108$ against $+0.326$ and median $v_z = -0.004$ against $-0.427$:
\textbf{annihilation photons entering horizontally near the sphere's equator}.
The model carries exactly one pinned line, at $10^{-0.2916} = 511$\,keV. It has
the line and it has the angular distribution; what it lacks is their joint. The
energy head and the geometry heads are coupled only through an 8-dimensional
latent, and this combination falls through it.

\begin{callout}{warn}{red!4}{This hole does not explain the detector deficit}
The under-covered events are \emph{depleted} among aimed crossings --- enrichment
$0.87\times$, carrying $2.38\,\%$ of the aimed population against $2.73\,\%$ of
the whole. The most prominent mismatch is not where the detector looks. The
conversion deficit is therefore characterised (diffuse, joint, coverage-flavoured,
resistant to reweighting) but \textbf{not mechanistically explained}. Do not
present the 511\,keV hole as its cause.
\end{callout}

\paragraph{What this unlocks.} Every architecture change until now has been judged
on marginals, or on a downstream $R$ carrying $\pm0.09$. The classifier AUC is
sensitive to the joint, costs minutes, and resolves differences far below what
$R$ can. \texttt{energy\_condition\_geometry} --- conditioning the energy head on
sampled geometry, the exact mechanism this defect calls for --- already exists as
the v0.8.3 feature that was implemented, tested and not adopted
(\S\ref{sec:v083}). It had never been evaluated against this metric. That test
was brought forward from September and launched 19/08; see \S\ref{sec:coupling}.

\subsection{Memorisation --- does MAGI generate, or recall?}
\label{sec:memorisation}

The KDSource benchmark showed a $287\times$ amplification does not by itself
distinguish MAGI from a kernel resampler. This test does. For each generated
sample, the nearest-neighbour distance to a reference set of real crossings, in
the model's own feature space with the azimuths carried as $(\cos,\sin)$ pairs so
the metric respects periodicity. Memorisation puts generated samples closer to
training points than real data lies from itself.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
Arm & \multicolumn{2}{c}{DM1.2} & \multicolumn{2}{c}{CR (ingoing-fixed)} \\
    & ratio & near-dup & ratio & near-dup \\
\midrule
real $\to$ real (LOO)      & 1.000 & $1.00\,\%$ & 1.000 & $1.00\,\%$ \\
held-out real \emph{(control)} & 0.999 & $1.07\,\%$ & 1.001 & $0.90\,\%$ \\
\textbf{MAGI}              & \textbf{1.536} & $\mathbf{0.34\,\%}$ & \textbf{1.027} & $\mathbf{0.89\,\%}$ \\
KDE, Silverman             & 4.123 & $0.00\,\%$ & 2.028 & $0.00\,\%$ \\
KDE, adaptive kNN          & 5.304 & $0.00\,\%$ & 3.999 & $0.00\,\%$ \\
memoriser, jitter 0 \emph{(control)} & 0.000 & $100\,\%$ & 0.000 & $100\,\%$ \\
memoriser, jitter 0.02     & 0.399 & $9.0\,\%$ & 0.207 & $99.9\,\%$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\textbf{MAGI does not memorise.} Both ratios exceed 1, and both near-duplicate
fractions are \emph{below} real data's own --- MAGI reproduces training points
less often than real crossings coincide with each other. Recall drives the ratio
down, and no point on the memoriser curve approaches these values.

The metric is calibrated in both directions before being trusted: held-out real
data reads as real ($0.999$/$1.001$, KS $p=0.81$/$0.96$) and literal copies read
as pure recall ($0.000$, $100\,\%$). That two-sided validation is what makes the
MAGI numbers interpretable.

CR at $1.027$ is materially better than DM1.2 at $1.536$: the CR model sits
essentially where real data sits, while DM1.2 is over-dispersed by $\sim$$54\,\%$
in nearest-neighbour distance --- the opposite failure from memorisation, and
consistent with its 5--11\,\% marginal defects. KDSource is $2$--$5\times$ further
off the manifold in both bandwidth modes, which is the trade behind its
correlation win and its $15.45\,\%$ physically-impossible samples.

\begin{callout}{warn}{red!4}{Controls that failed first, and were fixed}
The KDE arm was originally introduced as ``a memoriser by construction''. It is
not: at Silverman bandwidth in 7-D the kernel is $\sim$$4\times$ the
nearest-neighbour spacing, so it over-smooths. A second control at jitter $0.02$
was then judged against a hand-picked $50\,\%$ near-duplicate threshold, which
its own displacement ($0.053$) could not reach past $\varepsilon=0.033$. Both were
replaced by a calibration curve. Neither error survived into a result, because
the script refused to conclude while its negative control was unvalidated.
\end{callout}


\section{Cost --- measured on DM1.2 and on SRON}
\label{sec:cost}

The cost claim must not be an absolute wall-clock number, which depends on the
machine. It must be a \emph{ratio} with both sides measured on the same
hardware, and it must be decomposed --- because the naive figure is wrong by two
orders of magnitude. DM1.2 was long the only case where both sides ran here,
since the SRON reference ran on a cluster \ev{E15}. As of 15 August 2026 SRON
has been measured directly, by re-running \emph{both} arms locally back to back
(\S\ref{sec:cost-sron}) \ev{E18}.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrl}
\toprule
Step & wall & jobs & core-h & output \\
\midrule
full simulation, $2\times10^{8}$ muons & 12.71\,h & 8 & \textbf{101.67}
  & \num{999358} crossings, \num{1733} events \\
MAGI generation, $1.2\times10^{6}$ & 31\,s & 6 & 0.05 & --- \\
MAGI training, 3 seeds & 486/620/743\,s & 1 & 0.51 & --- \\
MAGI injection, $1.2\times10^{6}$ & 9.74\,h & 6 & \textbf{58.46}
  & \num{2019} events \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{The efficiency factor is not the speed-up.} This is the trap, and it
belongs in the paper explicitly because a referee will look for it.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
Detector events per thrown muon & \num{8.665e-6} \\
Detector events per injected crossing & \num{1.683e-3} \\
\textbf{Statistical amplification} & \textbf{194.2$\times$} (ideal $1/\chi = 200.13$) \\
\midrule
core-h per thrown muon & \num{5.084e-7} \\
core-h per injected crossing & \num{4.872e-5} \\
\textbf{Transport cost ratio} & \textbf{95.8$\times$} \\
\midrule
\textbf{Net throughput gain} & $\mathbf{194.2/95.8 = 2.03\times}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Cross-checked by equivalent statistics: $1.2\times10^{6}$ crossings carry
$2.402\times10^{8}$ equivalent muons, so MAGI delivers \num{4.11e6}
equiv-muons/core-h against the full simulation's \num{1.97e6} ---
$2.09\times$, consistent. A crossing costs $\sim$96$\times$ more to transport
than a thrown muon \emph{because it starts on the sphere, inside the assembly,
in material and adjacent to the detector}, whereas most of the $2\times10^{8}$
thrown muons miss the apparatus and are killed almost immediately. MAGI removes
the cheapest transport in the problem.

\paragraph{General form, and why DM1.2 is the pessimistic case.}
\begin{equation}
\text{speed-up} \;=\; \frac{1}{\chi} \times
\frac{c_{\mathrm{primary}}}{c_{\mathrm{crossing}}}
\end{equation}
\textbf{$c_{\mathrm{primary}}$ here is the cost per primary of the
\emph{whole} run, outer and inner together} --- $2\times10^{8} \times
\num{5.084e-7} = 101.7$ core-h, the full simulation. With that reading
$\chi^{-1}c_{\mathrm{primary}}/c_{\mathrm{crossing}}$ is
$C_{\mathrm{total}}/C_{\mathrm{inner}}$, and the equation returns the throughput
gain directly. Substituting an \emph{outer-only} cost per primary instead
returns $C_{\mathrm{outer}}/C_{\mathrm{inner}}$, which is smaller by exactly
one; see \S\ref{sec:cost-sron}, where that is the difference between
$2.33\times$ and $3.33\times$.

One full run decomposes into outer transport (primary\,$\to$\,sphere,
\textbf{eliminated} by MAGI) 53.0 core-h, $52\,\%$; and inner transport
(sphere\,$\to$\,detector, \textbf{still paid}) 48.7 core-h, $48\,\%$. The gain
is set by how much material sits \emph{outside} the CryoSphere relative to
inside. DM1.2 is close to the worst case: a small laboratory cryostat with an
$R=95$\,mm sphere enclosing most of the assembly. For a spacecraft primaries
traverse metres of structure while the sphere-to-detector distance stays at
centimetres, so the gain should be far larger --- now measured on SRON, where
the outer share rises from $52\,\%$ to $70\,\%$ (\S\ref{sec:cost-sron}).

\paragraph{Methodological cross-check.} The inner cost per crossing is measured
twice, independently: \num{4.8731e-5} core-h from the full-simulation arm
(48.7 core-h / \num{999358} crossings) and \num{4.8717e-5} from the MAGI
injection arm (58.46 / $1.2\times10^{6}$). They agree to $0.03\,\%$. The
two-arm decomposition is therefore not an assumption; it is verified.

\paragraph{Reuse across design variants --- where the speed-up lives.}
The decisive property is that the CryoSphere crossing population is determined
\emph{entirely by the geometry outside the sphere}. Design iteration changes the
inside --- detector layout, filters, readout electronics, shielding around the
absorber --- and leaves the outer structure untouched, so the training set stays
valid across the whole campaign and the full simulation is paid \textbf{once},
not once per variant. For $N$ inner-design variants at reference-equivalent
statistics: full simulation $N \times 101.7$ core-h; MAGI
$102.2 + N \times 48.7$ core-h.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{rrrr}
\toprule
$N$ variants & full sim & MAGI & speed-up \\
\midrule
1  & 102   & 151   & 0.67$\times$ \\
2  & 203   & 200   & 1.02$\times$ \\
5  & 508   & 346   & 1.47$\times$ \\
10 & 1{,}017 & 589 & 1.73$\times$ \\
20 & 2{,}033 & 1{,}076 & 1.89$\times$ \\
$\infty$ & & & \textbf{2.09$\times$} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\textbf{Break-even at $N \approx 1.93$ --- the second design variant.} This is
the same arithmetic as ``1.93$\times$ more statistics of the same geometry'',
but it means something entirely different: nobody wants to re-run an identical
geometry for twice the statistics, whereas testing a second inner configuration
is what a design campaign does on day one. For a flight model, where the outer
share exceeds DM1.2's $52\,\%$, the break-even approaches $N=1$ --- MAGI pays
for itself on the \emph{first} redesign.

\paragraph{The claim that does not depend on compute at all.}
MAGI converts a \textbf{statistically exhausted} sample into an unlimited one.
The $^{40}$K training set holds \num{1873609} real crossings and more cannot be
had without re-running an enormous campaign; MAGI generated \num{538205667} from
it, a \textbf{287$\times$ amplification}, and the generated population closes at
the detector at $1.118 \pm 0.373$. No amount of compute buys that from the
original sample.

The arithmetic is independent of \emph{which} population was amplified, so the
$287\times$ stands as stated. But that population is the albedo set of
\S\ref{sec:k40}, so the sentence to use is ``MAGI amplifies an exhausted sample
and the amplified population closes at detector level'' --- a statement about
the method --- and \emph{not} ``MAGI reproduces the $^{40}$K background''.
Until the September regeneration, DM1.2 and CR carry every physics claim.

\section{Correction: the ``seed systematic'' is withdrawn}
\label{sec:seed}

\begin{callout}{warn}{red!4}{Withdrawn claim}
Earlier versions of the internal documents reported that MAGI's
detector-relevant energy ratio spans $0.455$--$1.222$ across training seeds, and
recommended quoting a $\pm40\,\%$ single-seed / $\pm15\,\%$ ensembled
systematic. \textbf{That spread is an artefact of the metric and is
withdrawn.} It was never measured on a physical quantity.
\end{callout}

The metric \texttt{energy\_vs\_impact\_parameter} compares median generated to
median real energy among detector-aimed crossings. On the CR source that
statistic estimates the \emph{species mix}, not the energy conditional.

At $b<10$\,mm the real population is \ev{E12}: $\mu^{-}$ $48.008\,\%$ at median
\SI{828.373}{\mega\electronvolt}; $\gamma$ $49.789\,\%$ at median
\SI{0.511}{\mega\electronvolt}; $e^{-}$ $2.092\,\%$ at median
\SI{3.170}{\mega\electronvolt}; aggregate median
\SI{13.288}{\mega\electronvolt}. The two dominant populations are three orders
of magnitude apart, split almost exactly 50/50, and \emph{disjoint} ($\gamma$
99th percentile \SI{30.488}{\mega\electronvolt}, $\mu^{-}$ 1st percentile
\SI{24.072}{\mega\electronvolt}). The 50th percentile of the combined sample
therefore falls in the empty gap between them, and the aggregate median is
close to a step function of the muon fraction.

\paragraph{Why the v0.8.3 checkpoints could not answer this.}
The three v0.8.3 runs \ev{E13} moved \texttt{latent\_dim} $8\to12$ alongside the
flag, and were trained on the \emph{pre-ingoing-fix} CR set --- their configs
record \texttt{n\_types}~=~6 against the corrected set's~4. They are therefore
neither a controlled test of the coupling nor trained on the data the current
result rests on, whichever metric they are scored by. The A/B below is the first
controlled test the flag has had.

\paragraph{Design.} \texttt{run\_coupling\_ab.py} builds the dataset \emph{once}
--- features, gate targets, quantile transformers, split, conditioning, tf.data
pipelines --- and trains two models on that identical object: 40 epochs, seed 42,
M1 CPU, \num{2616145} training rows. \texttt{energy\_condition\_geometry} is the
only line that differs. The Colab reference checkpoint is re-scored alongside
both, so device and seed variation between it and the two local models is
measured rather than assumed. Scoring is classifier AUC against \num{400000} real
crossings: sensitive to the joint, and with negligible statistical error next to
the $\pm0.09$ that $R$ carries.

\paragraph{The implementation is sound.} \texttt{core/model.py} factorises
$p(\mathrm{geom}\mid z)\,p(E\mid\mathrm{geom},z)$ with teacher forcing on true
targets during training and re-decoding from \emph{sampled} geometry at
generation, so training and generation apply the same conditional. The geometry
heads never see \texttt{geom\_obs}, so nothing leaks into their own
reconstruction term.

\paragraph{Third CR arm.} \ev{E20} The coupled checkpoint goes through the same
detector chain as the baseline, in \texttt{cr\_arm\_coupled/} --- a separate
directory, so the reported campaign of \S\ref{sec:armAB} is untouched and stays
reproducible from its own. The real arm is \textbf{not} re-run:
\texttt{job\_real\_*} is reused verbatim, so all three arms share one reference
exactly and the Geant4 cost is halved to three jobs. Generation seeds
($9200+53j$) and Geant4 seeds ($k=3,4,5$) are the baseline MAGI arm's, so the
coupled arm differs from \texttt{job\_magi\_*} in exactly one thing: the
checkpoint.

Because both MAGI arms share the real arm their $R$ values are correlated, and
the gap between their error bars is not the error on their difference. The
reported statistic is therefore
\[
Q \;=\; \frac{(\text{events}/\text{crossing})_{\rm coupled}}
             {(\text{events}/\text{crossing})_{\rm baseline}},
\]
in which the real arm cancels entirely, and which is paired on the Geant4 side as
well. $Q = 1$ means coupling changed nothing at the detector.

\begin{callout}{warn}{red!4}{This arm is additive, and it runs unconditionally}
The production model stays \texttt{v0\_8\_2\_CR\_ingoingfix}. The memorisation
test (\S\ref{sec:memorisation}), the classifier test (\S\ref{sec:classifier}),
the reweighting and arm~A (\S\ref{sec:armAB}) are all keyed to that checkpoint;
swapping it would invalidate four completed results to chase one unproven
mechanism, five days before the meeting.

The arm also runs \emph{regardless} of the AUC verdict. Gating it on a favourable
AUC would select exactly the cases that cannot test the AUC~$\to R$ link: a null
AUC with a moved $Q$, or the reverse, is the informative outcome, and only an
unconditional run can find it. \textbf{This is now the operative case}: the AUC
verdict is null, and only the third arm can say whether $Q$ agrees.
\end{callout}

An AUC improvement is \textbf{not} licence to quote an improved $R$. AUC is a
distributional distance on the crossing surface; $R$ is an integrated detector
rate after inner transport. That the first implies the second is the hypothesis
under test, not a premise --- which is the whole reason the third arm exists.

\paragraph{One limit to read the result against.} The CR gate carries
\texttt{zones}~=~2: continuum plus a single pinned line at 511\,keV. There is
less for the coupling to express here than on a line-rich source, so a null on CR
bounds the mechanism on \emph{this} source rather than in general.

running, chained behind the scorer, launched \ev{E20}; results expected

\section{Summary --- the basis for the paper}
\label{sec:summary}

\subsection*{The claim}

MAGI is a conditional VAE that learns the phase-space distribution of particles
crossing a closed surface around a cryogenic detector, so that the expensive
transport from the outer mass model down to that surface is paid once and the
learned distribution is resampled thereafter. The paper's contribution is not
that the marginals are reproduced --- that is table stakes for a generative
model --- but that the surrogate is validated \textbf{downstream}: its output is
pushed through the real Geant4 mass model and the resulting \emph{detector}
spectrum is compared with the full simulation. That closure is rare in the ML
literature and is the strongest thing here.

\subsection*{Fidelity of the generated population}

Correlation residual mean $|\Delta\rho| = 0.015$ (CR), 0.013 ($^{40}$K), 0.018
(DM1.2); species fractions to $0.3\,\%$; energy marginals within a few percent
per decade. \textbf{Geometry marginals are reproduced to 5--11\,\% RMS per bin},
against a $0.44\,\%$ Poisson floor --- a real, characterized limitation, and the
number to quote rather than any claim of sub-percent geometric fidelity.

\subsection*{Cost --- state it carefully}

The efficiency factor is \emph{statistical amplification}, not compute saving.
On DM1.2, measured with both arms on the same machine: amplification
$194\times$ (ideal 200.13), transport cost ratio $95.8\times$, net throughput
gain $\mathbf{2.03\times}$. One full run splits $52\,\%$ outer / $48\,\%$ inner;
MAGI eliminates the outer only, so
$\text{speed-up} = \chi^{-1}\,c_{\rm primary}/c_{\rm crossing}$.

On SRON, the flight-like model, both arms were re-run locally and the gain is
larger: outer share $70\,\%$, \textbf{speed-up $3.33\times$}, break-even at
$N = 1.43$ variants. Note that \texttt{run\_speedup.sh} prints $2.33\times$ for
this, which is $C_{\rm outer}/C_{\rm inner}$ and not the speed-up
(\S\ref{sec:cost-sron}); both SRON and DM1.2 figures are lower bounds, since
Building\,$\to$\,Dome is excluded.

The argument that carries the paper is \textbf{reuse}: the crossing population is
fixed by the geometry \emph{outside} the sphere, so a design campaign that
iterates the inner assembly pays the full simulation once. Break-even is the
\textbf{second design variant} on both geometries. The prediction that a
flight-like model would do better than DM1.2 is no longer an assertion --- it is
measured, $52\,\% \to 70\,\%$ outer share. Separately,
MAGI amplified an exhausted $^{40}$K sample $287\times$ ---
$1.87\times10^{6} \to 5.38\times10^{8}$ crossings --- which no amount of compute
can buy from the original sample. State it as amplification \emph{of a sample};
that sample is albedo (\S\ref{sec:k40}).

\subsection*{Limitations to state in the paper}

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{The CR $15$--$24\,\%$ deficit is not fully explained.}
        $3.6\,\%$ is attributed to an inconsistent ingoing-particle cut; the
        remainder is uncharacterized since the energy$\times$geometry diagnosis
        was withdrawn (\S\ref{sec:seed}).
  \item \textbf{A direction-conditional species-mix error of $\sim$5\,\%.}
        Global species fractions are right to $0.3\,\%$, but conditioned on
        pointing at the detector the mix is off by 5--9\,\%. On DM1.2 this
        produces a coherent $4\,\%$ deficit in deposited energy, traced to
        incidence angle (median $\sec\theta$ 0.955 of truth).
  \item \textbf{A detector-level seed systematic of order $10\,\%$}, now
        measured (\S\ref{sec:inprogress}): three seeds, all-deposits channel,
        excess dispersion $9.1\,\%$ over a $3.4\,\%$ Poisson floor,
        $p = 2.2\times10^{-4}$, with the channel chosen post hoc and only 2
        d.o.f. behind the estimate. The previously reported $\pm40\,\%$ remains
        withdrawn --- it was an artefact of a metric that measures the species
        mix on a bimodal source, and this result is an order of magnitude
        smaller and in a different channel. It is the right size to be the
        already-documented $\pm5\,\%$ aimed-mix error propagating downstream.
  \item \textbf{SRON and DM1.2 are on different ingoing conventions} --- the
        SRON training sets contain $10.4\,\%$ ($^{40}$K: $22.5\,\%$) outgoing
        crossings, DM1.2 none.
  \item \textbf{Cost is now measured on two geometries}, DM1.2 ($2.03\times$,
        the pessimistic case) and SRON ($3.33\times$). Both exclude the
        Building\,$\to$\,Dome transport and are therefore lower bounds. Neither
        is the Athena flight mass model.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{A methodological point worth making}

\texttt{energy\_vs\_impact\_parameter} passed its own self-verification cleanly
(1.000 on identical inputs, 0.050 on an injected anti-correlation) while being
the wrong estimator for the population it was applied to. Self-verification
proves a metric computes what it computes; it cannot reveal that the statistic
is inappropriate. On a bimodal multi-species source the aggregate median is a
knife-edge function of the species mix --- a $\pm5\,\%$ mix shift swings it over
0.70--1.60 with \emph{zero} energy error. This cost a development cycle and is
worth stating positively: it tells anyone reusing the method which check to run,
and it is not visible in any diagnostic the field would normally apply.

\subsection*{Release readiness}

Against the seven-layer picture: layers 1, 2 and 5 are strong (5 unusually so);
layer 3 has one hole (no classifier two-sample test) that is now known to be
load-bearing, since the defect found here is exactly what such a test detects;
layer 4 has no memorization test while the paper quotes a $287\times$
amplification; layer 6 lost its strongest entry with the seed withdrawal;
layer 7 is measured on DM1.2 only. \textbf{Layer 0 --- LICENSE, tag, DOI --- is
not started and blocks release.}
