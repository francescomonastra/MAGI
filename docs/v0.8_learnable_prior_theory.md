# MAGI v0.8 — learnable prior: mathematical foundations & scientific context

Companion to `docs/v0.8_learnable_prior_plan.md` (intuition + implementation)
and `docs/v0.8_fixing_plan.md` §8 (the empirical z-leak result). This note
supplies the analytical backbone: *why* the aggregated-posterior/prior mismatch
is guaranteed to arise, *why it scales with decoder-head capacity* (so it hit
the energy flow and not the geometry Gaussians), *why a learnable prior provably
fixes it*, and where all of this sits in the literature.

Notation: data event `x=(E, g)` (energy + geometry), source/type label `c`
(`cond`), latent `z ∈ R^d`, `d=8`. Encoder `q_φ(z|x,c)`, decoder `p_θ(x|z,c)`,
prior `p(z|c)`. Empirical data law `p_data(x|c)`. All `E_x[·]` are over
`p_data`. We drop `c` when a statement holds per-condition.

---

## 1. The ELBO and the two gaps

Per-event evidence lower bound:

    log p(x) ≥ L(x) = E_{q_φ(z|x)}[log p_θ(x|z)] − KL(q_φ(z|x) ‖ p(z)).           (1)

The slack in (1) is the **posterior-approximation gap**

    log p(x) − L(x) = KL(q_φ(z|x) ‖ p_θ(z|x)) ≥ 0,                                 (2)

i.e. how far the amortized Gaussian encoder is from the true posterior. IAF on
the *encoder* attacks (2). It is **not** the generation bug — keep it separate.

---

## 2. ELBO surgery: the aggregated-posterior identity

Define the **aggregated (marginal) posterior**

    q_φ(z) := E_{x∼p_data}[ q_φ(z|x) ] = (1/N) Σ_n q_φ(z|x_n).                      (3)

This is the actual distribution of latent codes the decoder is trained on.
Averaging the KL term of (1) over the data and inserting/removing `log q_φ(z)`:

    E_x KL(q_φ(z|x) ‖ p(z))
      = E_x E_{q_φ(z|x)}[log q_φ(z|x) − log q_φ(z)]        ← = I(x;z)
      + E_x E_{q_φ(z|x)}[log q_φ(z)   − log p(z)]          ← = KL(q_φ(z) ‖ p(z))

hence the **Hoffman–Johnson (2016) identity**

    E_x KL(q_φ(z|x) ‖ p(z)) = I(x;z) + KL(q_φ(z) ‖ p(z)).                          (4)

- `I(x;z)` is the mutual information between data and code under the joint
  `p_data(x) q_φ(z|x)` — the *useful* information the latent carries.
- `KL(q_φ(z) ‖ p(z))` is the **marginal mismatch**: how far the aggregated
  posterior is from the prior we sample at generation.

The full averaged objective is therefore

    L̄ = E_x E_{q_φ(z|x)}[log p_θ(x|z)] − I(x;z) − KL(q_φ(z) ‖ p(z)).              (5)

Read (5) carefully: with a **fixed** prior `p(z)=N(0,I)`, the model can lower
the objective only by (i) shrinking `I(x;z)` (posterior collapse — discards
information, hurts reconstruction) or (ii) reshaping `q_φ(z)` toward `N(0,I)`.
For structured data and a finite-capacity encoder, `q_φ(z)` **cannot** equal
`N(0,I)`, so there is an **irreducible floor**

    KL(q_φ(z) ‖ N(0,I)) ≥ ε > 0.                                                   (6)

The fixed-prior VAE is structurally unable to close this gap.

---

## 3. Why the gap breaks *generation* specifically (and reconstruction doesn't notice)

Two distributions over `x` are in play:

- **Aggregated-decoder law** (what training's reconstruction term actually
  shapes toward `p_data`, because reconstruction only ever feeds the decoder
  `z ∼ q_φ`):

      p_θ^q(x) := ∫ p_θ(x|z) q_φ(z) dz.                                            (7)

- **Generative law** (what `generate()` produces, sampling `z ∼ p(z)`):

      p_θ^p(x) := ∫ p_θ(x|z) p(z) dz.                                              (8)

Both share the *same* decoder kernel `p_θ(x|z)`. The data-processing inequality
for KL under a common Markov kernel gives the clean bound

    KL( p_θ^q(x) ‖ p_θ^p(x) ) ≤ KL( q_φ(z) ‖ p(z) ).                              (9)

**This is the theorem that justifies the whole fix.** The left side is the
generation error — the divergence between "what reconstruction made faithful"
(7) and "what you actually sample" (8). It is upper-bounded by the marginal
mismatch (the same quantity in (4)). Two consequences:

1. With `p(z)=N(0,I)`, (6) lower-bounds the *right* side, so the generation
   error has a floor you cannot train away by improving reconstruction.
2. The failure mode is **prior holes**: regions `A` with `p(z|A)` large but
   `q_φ(z|A)≈0`. On `A` the decoder had no training signal (reconstruction
   never sent `z` there), so `p_θ(x|z)` extrapolates arbitrarily. Sampling
   `z∼N(0,I)` lands in `A`; the decoded events are garbage. Note the relevant
   direction for *coverage* is the mass of the prior outside `q_φ`'s support
   (`KL(p‖q)`-like), which the ELBO’s `KL(q‖p)` term does **not** even directly
   penalize — a small ELBO KL can coexist with severe holes. That asymmetry is
   why the bug is invisible in the loss curve.

**Reconstruction/NLL keep improving while generation degrades.** Val-NLL is
computed with `z∼q_φ(z|x)` (real posterior samples), so it tracks (7) and keeps
falling. Generation uses `z∼N(0,I)` and tracks (8). As training pushes `q_φ(z)`
further from `N(0,I)`, the right side of (9) grows and the two curves diverge —
exactly the 5-epoch-good / 150-epoch-worse behavior logged in
`v0.8_fixing_plan.md` §8.

## 4. Why the *energy flow* leaks but the *geometry Gaussians* don't — a capacity argument

This is the part specific to MAGI, and (5) explains it quantitatively. Split the
per-event reconstruction gain against its information cost. A decoder head gains
likelihood by making `p_θ(x|z)` depend on `z`; the marginal return is

    ∂/∂[coupling] { E_x E_{q}[log p_θ(x|z)] − I(x;z) }.

A **high-capacity** head (the conditional RQS **energy flow**) can realize a
large increase in `E_q[log p_θ]` per unit of `I(x;z)`: it can carve a sharply
different 1-D energy density for nearby `z`, effectively storing "this event's
energy" in `z` and reading it back at reconstruction. So the optimizer is driven
to **high `I(x;z)`** through the energy head — a very informative, peaky
`q_φ(z|x)`, hence a highly structured, multi-lobed `q_φ(z)` far from `N(0,I)`,
hence large `KL(q_φ(z)‖N(0,I))` and deep holes. Via (9), generation blows up —
your measured **peak-core 0.39×, far-above-peak 697×** at 150 epochs, and the
gate over-routing to lines (`[0.65,0.18,0.17]` vs true `[0.97,0.015,0.015]`) are
the decoded-hole artifacts.

A **low-capacity** head (the geometry **Gaussian** `N(μ(z),σ(z))`) has a poor
likelihood-per-bit ratio: an affine-in-features mean can’t convert latent
information into much NLL, so at the optimum it uses `z` mildly, `q_φ(z|x)` stays
broad, `q_φ(z)` stays near `N(0,I)`, the residual `ε` is small, and (9) stays
tight. Same prior, same encoder — **the mismatch is amplified by whichever head
is most capable of exploiting `z`.** That is why swapping the categorical/Gaussian
energy head for a flow is precisely what *exposed* a mismatch that was always
latent in the architecture.

Corollary (matches §8 finding 2 and the `w_gate_aux` result): raising an
auxiliary weight that *sharpens the training-latent gate* only increases
`I(x;z)` further, worsening `KL(q_φ(z)‖p)` and thus generation. You cannot fix a
marginal-mismatch problem with a per-event (posterior-side) knob.

The `cond`-only ablation (flow and gate on `c`, not `z`) works in §8 because it
forces `I(x;z)=0` for the energy head — no leak, no holes — but it also sets the
energy↔geometry coupling to zero, which the physics forbids. So the ablation is
not a fix; it is a proof that the leak lives in the `z` path.

## 5. The learnable prior: the provable fix

Make the prior parametric and trainable: `p_ψ(z|c)`, a **conditional coupling /
spline flow**. Only the last term of (5) depends on `ψ`, so training the prior
minimizes `KL(q_φ(z|c)‖p_ψ(z|c))`. Two exact facts:

**(i) Optimal prior = aggregated posterior.** For fixed encoder, the prior that
maximizes the ELBO is

    p*(z|c) = q_φ(z|c),   giving  KL(q_φ‖p*) = 0.                                  (10)

(Immediate from (5): the `ψ`-dependent term is `−KL(q_φ(z)‖p_ψ)`, maximized at
equality.) A flexible `p_ψ` approximates (10) within its family. This is the
theoretical statement behind VampPrior (Tomczak & Welling 2018), which sets
`p(z)=\frac1K Σ_k q_φ(z|u_k)` — an explicit mixture approximation to `q_φ(z)`.

**(ii) Generation becomes exact in the bound.** Substituting (10) into (9),
`KL(p_θ^q ‖ p_θ^p) → 0`: sampling `z∼p_ψ≈q_φ(z)` feeds the decoder **exactly the
code distribution it was trained on**. Holes vanish by construction, for **every
head at once** (energy and geometry), because (9) is a statement about the shared
`z`, not about any one head.

**Crucially, coupling is retained.** Nothing above asks `I(x;z)` to shrink — the
energy flow may keep its full `z`-dependence (the physical energy↔geometry
correlation Francesco requires). The learnable prior removes the *penalty* that
high `I(x;z)` used to impose on generation. You keep the coupling **and** get
faithful samples. That is the exact resolution of the §8 design tension.

**Conditional case.** The identity and (10) hold per-`c`: the optimum is the
per-source aggregated posterior `q_φ(z|c)=E_{x∼p_data(·|c)}[q_φ(z|x,c)]`. A
`cond`-conditioned prior flow is therefore the right object — it matches each
source’s latent law, consistent with the per-source continuum + coupling picture
of §8.

### 5a. Why the MC-KL estimator uses the *mass-covering* direction (and that’s the point)

The plan’s estimator (`v0.8_learnable_prior_plan.md` §6b)

    kl_per = log q_φ(z|x) − log p_ψ(z),     z ∼ q_φ(z|x)  (reparameterized)       (11)

is an unbiased single-sample estimate of `KL(q_φ(z|x)‖p_ψ(z))`, and averaged over
the batch it trains `p_ψ` by **maximizing `E_{z∼q_φ} log p_ψ(z)`**, i.e.
minimizing the **forward** KL `KL(q_φ(z)‖p_ψ)`. Forward KL is **mass-covering**:
`p_ψ` is pushed to put mass wherever `q_φ` has mass, which is exactly the
property that eliminates prior holes (a mode-seeking reverse-KL prior could leave
holes uncovered). So the naive-looking estimator is, by good fortune of the ELBO
algebra, the *right* divergence direction for the generation objective. Variance
is higher than the closed-form Gaussian KL but is controlled by minibatching;
keep the closed form for `prior="gaussian"`.

### 5b. Joint-training dynamics (a caveat to watch)

Encoder and prior move together: as `p_ψ` chases `q_φ(z)`, the marginal penalty
in (5) becomes cheap to satisfy, which *removes the regularization pressure* on
`I(x;z)` and lets the encoder encode even more. Usually beneficial (more retained
information, better reconstruction), but it can drift toward an over-informative
latent / near-deterministic encoder. Mitigations, in increasing order of
robustness: (a) a mild `β<1` or free-bits floor on the posterior KL to keep
`q_φ(z|x)` from collapsing to a point; (b) train the prior on a slightly slower
schedule; (c) the **two-stage** route (Dai & Wipf 2019): freeze the VAE, then fit
`p_ψ` to the frozen `{z_n∼q_φ(z|x_n)}` as a pure density-estimation problem. Two
-stage is the lowest-risk option for a beta — it cannot destabilize the decoder,
it reuses the trained v0.7.x/v0.8 model unchanged, and it directly targets (10).
Recommend implementing `p_ψ` so it supports **both** joint and post-hoc fitting
from the same module.

## 6. Scientific context / where this sits in the literature

Diagnosis of the aggregated-posterior/prior mismatch:
- Hoffman & Johnson (2016), *ELBO surgery* — identity (4); the origin of the
  "prior should match the aggregated posterior" view.
- Rosca, Lakshminarayanan & Mohamed (2018), *Distribution Matching in VAEs* —
  empirical demonstration of aggregated-posterior holes and sample degradation.
- Makhzani et al. (2016), *Adversarial Autoencoders* — an encoder-side fix:
  match `q_φ(z)` to `p(z)` adversarially instead of learning the prior.

Learnable / expressive priors (the fix family):
- Tomczak & Welling (2018), *VAE with a VampPrior* — mixture-of-posteriors prior;
  the explicit realization of (10).
- Chen et al. (2017), *Variational Lossy Autoencoder* — autoregressive prior.
- Bauer & Mnih (2019), *Resampled Priors for VAEs* — learned accept/reject over
  `N(0,I)`.
- Dai & Wipf (2019), *Diagnosing and Enhancing VAE Models* — the two-stage VAE;
  shows the `N(0,I)` mismatch is a primary cause of poor VAE sample quality and
  that fitting a second-stage density on the codes fixes it. **Most directly
  relevant to a low-risk beta.**
- Aneja et al. (2021), *NCP-VAE*; Vahdat & Kautz (2020), *NVAE* — noise-contrastive
  / flow priors in modern hierarchical VAEs.

Normalizing flows (the machinery for `p_ψ` and the energy continuum):
- Rezende & Mohamed (2015), *Variational Inference with Normalizing Flows*.
- Dinh et al. (2017), *RealNVP*; Kingma et al. (2016), *IAF*; Papamakarios et al.
  (2017), *MAF* — the coupling-vs-autoregressive direction trade-off used in
  `docs/v0.8_IAF_integration_plan.md` §5a.
- Durkan et al. (2019), *Neural Spline Flows* — the RQS transform MAGI already
  uses for the 1-D energy continuum (`core/flows.py`).
- Papamakarios et al. (2021), *Normalizing Flows for Probabilistic Modeling and
  Inference* (JMLR review).

Domain tie-in (worth a sentence in the paper):
- Krause & Shih (2021), *CaloFlow* — HEP calorimeter fast-sim moved from VAE/GAN
  to normalizing flows precisely for distribution-level fidelity and stable
  sampling. MAGI adopting a flow prior is the same methodological step in the
  particle-background-simulation setting, and connects directly to the CaloFlow /
  CaloGAN / AtlFast3 lineage already in `magi_references.bib`.

Foundations: Kingma & Welling (2014), *Auto-Encoding Variational Bayes*;
Rezende et al. (2014); Sohn et al. (2015), *Conditional VAE*.

## 7. One-paragraph summary for the paper (Track B methods)

A conditional VAE with a fixed `N(0,I)` prior minimizes, up to the mutual
information `I(x;z)`, the divergence `KL(q_φ(z)‖p(z))` between the aggregated
posterior and the prior (Eq. 4), but for structured data this divergence has a
strictly positive floor (Eq. 6). Because the generation error is upper-bounded by
exactly this divergence (Eq. 9), a fixed-prior CVAE has an irreducible
sampling-fidelity gap that reconstruction cannot close, realized as prior holes
that a high-capacity (flow) decoder head amplifies by driving `I(x;z)` up. Making
the prior a learnable conditional flow drives `KL(q_φ(z|c)‖p_ψ(z|c))→0` — its
optimum is the aggregated posterior itself (Eq. 10) — which closes the generation
gap for all decoder heads simultaneously while preserving the latent-mediated
energy↔geometry coupling required by the physics.
