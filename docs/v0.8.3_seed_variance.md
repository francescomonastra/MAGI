# The energy×geometry coupling is seed-variable — findings and the plan for the next version

Measured 12/08/2026 on CR. Written after `energy_condition_geometry` +
`latent_dim 8→12` was implemented, trained at three seeds, and **failed**.

**Decision taken: stay on v0.8.2 for the validation and the paper, quote the
seed systematic, and resume this work afterwards.**

---

## 1. Summary

MAGI reproduces every *marginal* of the CR crossing population to a few percent
and still gets the detector rate wrong by an amount that **depends on the
training seed**. The channel is the joint distribution of energy and aiming: how
hard a particle is, given that it points at the absorber. Marginal validation —
energy decades, species fractions, correlation matrices — is blind to it.

Across six independently trained checkpoints the detector-relevant ratio spans
**0.455 to 1.222** while every energy decade stays within a few percent in all
six.

This is a property of the *training run*, not of the architecture, and it is the
reason no single-seed measurement can demonstrate a fix.

---

## 2. The metric

`magi.energy_vs_impact_parameter(P_real, U_real, E_real, P_gen, U_gen, E_gen,
detector_centre, cuts)` — median generated energy divided by median real energy,
for crossings whose straight ray passes within `b` of the detector, restricted
to rays travelling toward it.

In the truth, **median energy is flat in `b`** (~12–13 MeV at every cut for CR):
energy and aiming are independent. Any trend in the ratio is model error.

Self-test: exactly 1.000 at every cut on identical inputs, 0.050 on an injected
anti-correlation.

**Two mistakes this metric is sensitive to, both of which bit during this
study:**

1. **Neutrinos.** `v0_8_2_priorzone_CR` was trained on a set that still contained
   them — its `type_probs` carry `anti_nu_e` and `nu_mu` at 2.3 % each — while
   `generation/export.py` drops them (`NON_TRANSPORT_PARTICLES`) when writing a
   Geant4 source. Sampling `type_probs` directly therefore yields ~4.6 %
   neutrinos the real pipeline never emits, which alone inflated the 10–100 MeV
   decade from 6.4 % to 10.6 % and corrupted every median-energy number. Always
   filter as `export.py` does.
2. **Unequal generated N.** Small-`b` bins hold ~0.5 % of events; comparing a
   380 k sample against a 1.6 M one is not a comparison. Use matched N and
   bootstrap CIs.

## 4. What v0.8.3 did and did not do

**Did not fix the target.** Spread at b < 10 mm: v0.8.2 0.495–1.222, v0.8.3
0.455–1.082. Essentially unchanged, and v0.8.3 `seed7` is as bad as the worst
v0.8.2 seed.

**Introduced a bias at small `b`.** At b < 5 mm all three v0.8.3 seeds are low —
0.665 [0.565, 0.809], 0.439 [0.392, 0.506], 0.625 [0.490, 0.842], every
bootstrap CI excluding 1. v0.8.2 scattered around 1 there (1.058, 0.841, 0.508).
**Trading variance for bias is a regression**, even though the numbers look
tidier.

**Regressed the low-energy marginals, consistently across all three seeds:**

| decade | v0.8.2 (42/7/13) | v0.8.3 (42/7/13) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.001–0.01 MeV | 0.880 / 0.921 / 0.995 | 0.677 / 0.715 / 0.738 |
| 0.01–0.1 MeV | 1.686 / 1.296 / 1.767 | 2.216 / 1.714 / 1.979 |

Everything from 0.1 MeV up stays within a few percent in both.

**Did help globally.** The "all ingoing" spread tightened from 0.813–1.290 to
0.822–1.006. So conditioning the energy head on geometry improves the aggregate
coupling and does nothing where it was needed.

---

## 5. Why it failed — the diagnosis that replaces the old one

The original hypothesis was a **latent bottleneck**: energy and geometry coupled
only through an 8-dim `z`, so the joint got squeezed. That predicted more
capacity plus an explicit factorization would fix it. It didn't.

The seed study points somewhere else: **the loss contains no term that covers
the detector-aimed subset.** Those crossings are ~15,000 of 1.9 M — 0.5 %, deep
in a tail of the joint. The objective is dominated by the bulk, so the model is
effectively unconstrained there and each run settles differently. Adding
capacity and a better factorization cannot help with something the objective
never asks about; it only changes *which* arbitrary answer a run reaches.

This also explains why the marginals are stable across seeds while this is not:
the marginals *are* what the loss optimizes.

## 6. Plan for the next version, ranked

### 6.1 Make the loss see the aimed subset — the actual fix

The impact parameter `b` is a differentiable function of the reconstructed
position and direction, so it can enter the objective directly.

- **(a) Importance weighting.** Weight each training event's reconstruction term
  by `w(b)`, upweighting small `b`. Cheapest possible change, no new machinery,
  directly makes the objective care. Risk: raises the variance of the gradient
  because few events carry large weight — cap the weight and monitor.
- **(b) A soft-binned conditional-moment penalty.** Assign events to `b` bins
  with a smooth kernel, then penalise the difference between generated and real
  **mean log E per bin**. Differentiable, cheap, and it targets exactly the
  quantity the metric reports. Prefer means or soft quantiles over medians.
- **(c) A rank-correlation penalty** on `logE` vs `b` — the most direct
  statement of "these should be independent", but a blunt instrument if the true
  relation is not exactly flat for another source. Check on K-40 first, whose
  spectrum is narrow.

Do (a) and (b) before (c). Validate each at **three seeds minimum**.

### 6.2 Seed ensembling — deployable now, and **measured**

Generate from N independently-trained checkpoints and pool at fixed *total* N.
`tools/pool_seeds.py`, 700,000 from each of three seeds (2.1e6 total, matching
the 2e6 single-model runs):

| cut | v0.8.2 ×3 pooled | v0.8.3 ×3 pooled |
|---|---|---|
| all ingoing | 1.078 [1.061, 1.094] | 0.933 [0.921, 0.946] |
| b < 50 mm | 1.273 [1.234, 1.312] | 1.003 [0.973, 1.034] |
| b < 20 mm | 1.107 [1.022, 1.196] | 0.831 [0.773, 0.879] |
| **b < 10 mm** | **0.898 [0.765, 1.068]** | 0.580 [0.528, 0.677] |

**It works, down to b ≈ 10 mm.** Pooled v0.8.2 gives 0.898 with a CI that
includes 1, against single seeds ranging 0.495–1.222. The spread really was
training stochasticity, and averaging over seeds removes most of it at no extra
generation cost. This is the recommended production practice until 6.1 lands:
**quote fluxes from a 3-seed ensemble, not a single checkpoint.**

Two limits, both important:

- **It does not rescue v0.8.3.** Pooled v0.8.3 is below 1 at every cut from
  b < 20 mm down, CIs excluding 1 — confirming that its small-`b` behaviour is a
  *bias*, which averaging cannot remove. Further evidence for staying on v0.8.2.
- **Below b ≈ 5 mm the bins are statistics-limited, not seed-limited.** Pooling
  at fixed total N adds no events there, and the pooled medians came out
  inconsistent with the constituent single-seed medians (which is impossible for
  equal-sized pools and therefore signals that ~60-event-per-model bins give an
  unstable median, not a real effect). **Do not quote b < 5 mm at these sample
  sizes.** Fixing that needs more generated events, not more seeds.

Residual after ensembling is still ~±15 % at b < 10 mm, so this mitigates the
systematic rather than removing it.

### 6.3 Metric-driven model selection

Now that the metric exists, train N seeds and select on the median-E-vs-`b`
profile computed on a **held-out** split, then evaluate on data untouched by the
selection. Legitimate, cheap, and immediately effective — but it manages the
symptom, so it does not replace 6.1.

### 6.4 Worth investigating, lower priority

- **Flow conditioner extrapolation.** At small `b` the conditioning input sits
  in a rare region; the RQS conditioner may extrapolate badly there. Try weight
  decay or a Lipschitz constraint on the conditioner.
- **A more physical selection variable.** `b` to a point is a proxy; the real
  quantity is ray-box acceptance on the actual sensitive volume. Using it makes
  the metric sharper at the cost of tying it to a mass model.
- **Detector-aware conditioning.** Feeding `b` in as an input feature would make
  the relation explicit, but ties the model to a detector position and hurts the
  generality that makes the surrogate worth having. Only if the above fail.

### 6.5 Process changes — adopt regardless

1. `energy_vs_impact_parameter` runs for **every** trained model, in the standard
   validation cell. Marginals alone are not evidence of a usable model.
2. **No flux number is quoted from a single seed.** Three minimum, spread
   reported.
3. Any generated-vs-real comparison filters neutrinos exactly as `export.py`
   does, and uses matched N with bootstrap CIs.

## 7. What to quote in the meantime

MAGI's detector-level flux prediction carries a **training-seed systematic** that
marginal validation does not reveal: across six checkpoints, 0.455–1.222 on the
detector-relevant energy ratio, with every energy decade stable to a few percent.
A 3-seed ensemble recovers 0.898 [0.765, 1.068] at b < 10 mm (§6.2), so the
practical statement is **~±15 % after ensembling, ~±40 % from a single seed**.

Any CR flux ratio in the note or the proceeding is one checkpoint's draw and must
carry its seed and this spread. See `MAGI_validation_note.md` §4b.1.

This is worth stating positively in the paper: it tells anyone reusing the method
which check to run, and it is not visible in any of the diagnostics the field
would normally apply.

## 8. Reproducing

```bash
# three seeds of a v0.8.3-style model
python tools/train_v083_cr_seed.py 7
python tools/train_v083_cr_seed.py 13

# matched-N comparison of any set of checkpoints
python tools/compare_v082_v083.py v0.8.2 v082-seed7 v082-seed13
python tools/compare_v082_v083.py v0.8.3 v083-seed7 v083-seed13

# seed-ensemble pooling
python tools/pool_seeds.py
```

The `energy_condition_geometry` flag stays in `core/model.py`, defaulting to
`False`, so v0.8.2 behaviour is bit-identical and the v0.8.3 experiment can be
resumed without re-implementing it.
