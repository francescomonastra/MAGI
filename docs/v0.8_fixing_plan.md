# MAGI v0.8 — fixing plan for the energy mixture head

Status: **§3(b), the conditional Normalizing Flow continuum, is now
IMPLEMENTED; the flow's continuum shape-capacity is proven on the single-peak
stress test (the §2b/§7 bottleneck is solved), but the physically-required
`z_cond` coupling default still needs a learnable prior for faithful
generation — read §8 first, it has the current bottom line and next steps.**
Sections 0-6 are the original design note (2026-07-21); §7 documents why
§3(a) (K-component Gaussian sub-mixture) was ruled out; §8 documents the flow
implementation, the three failure modes traced, the passing shape-capacity
result, Francesco's decision that energy<->geometry coupling must be
preserved (so a learnable prior is the real next step), and the ordered
to-do list. This file is self-contained; no need to re-read chat history.

## 0. Recap — how we got here

1. **Phase A** (shipped, commit `b11b34e`): `detect_energy_lines` +
   Geant4-grounded candidate line table (`tools/build_candidate_lines_from_geant4.py`,
   externalized as `CandidateLines/CANDIDATE_ENERGY_LINES_<mass_model>.json`).
   Confirmed real, detectable lines exist in CR/Small that the v0.7.2
   categorical energy head can't represent well.
2. **Phase B / Part 2**: built `CVAE_MixEnergy_ContPhi_TaskAdaptive` —
   `p(y|z,cond) = pi_0 * N(y; mu_c(z), sigma_c(z)) + sum_l pi_l * N(y; line_y[l], sigma_line)`,
   `y = T(E)`. Synthetic-verified, but the real run immediately hit a
   **gate-collapse degeneracy**: the encoder leaks "which line am I near"
   into `z`, so the continuum component alone can reconstruct everything and
   the gate never learns to route to lines. Fixed via **auxiliary gate
   supervision** (`build_gate_targets` — a physical-proximity soft target,
   independent of anything the model learns — pulls `softmax(gate_logits)`
   toward it; tuned to `w_gate_aux=0.3`, `continuum_floor=0.15`).
3. **First real CR+Small run** (gate-collapse fixed, everything else
   default): **catastrophically bad** — massive line over-generation (up to
   280x for weak lines) and large spurious continuum humps filling entire
   sparse low-energy regions with density that isn't there in real data.
   Diagnosed two causes:
   - `QuantileTransformer`'s local stretch (`dy/dE`) is
     **dataset-density-dependent** — measured 857 at the sparse end vs ~11
     in the dense bulk on Small (78x differential). Any small error in the
     continuum's per-sample mean gets amplified by exactly this factor when
     inverted back to E-space, landing in the emptiest real region.
   - Nothing stops the per-sample continuum mean `mu_c(z)` from drifting
     onto a line's position for latent codes near line-proximal training
     events — the *same* z-leakage degeneracy the gate fix solved, but for
     the continuum instead of the gate.
4. **Part 3 redesign** (this repo, `core/model.py::CVAE_MixEnergy_ContPhi_TaskAdaptive`):
   - `energy_transform="log10"` instead of `QuantileTransformer` (fixed,
     ~2.5-4x stretch differential instead of QT's dataset-dependent 78x).
   - New **continuum-repulsion regularizer**: `_continuum_repulsion(params)`
     penalizes `energy_cont_mu` for landing within `continuum_repulsion_margin`
     of any fixed line position, weighted by `w_continuum_repulsion`.
   - `beta` raised from 0.1 to reduce how much per-event "near a line" info
     `z` can carry.
   - Filter `matched_lines` to `count >= 100` before feeding the model
     (drops single-to-double-digit-count "matches", which are noise-level
     and were destabilizing the mixture).
   - Tuned on a **harder synthetic stress test** (multi-modal continuum +
     4 closely-spaced lines, 0.1 dex apart, in a sparse tail): after two
     tuning passes, landed on `beta=0.2`, `w_continuum_repulsion=0.3`,
     `continuum_repulsion_margin=0.05` — recovery ratios 0.47-0.72x (target
     was 0.5-2x), gate weights tracked true injected values reasonably,
     "between-line" windows near 1.0x. One residual: a single sparsest edge
     window still showed a 9x excess on a tiny absolute count (63 generated
     vs 7 real out of 30k) — judged acceptable at the time as a tail-shape
     approximation artifact, not the encoder/decoder degeneracy.
5. **Second real CR+Small run** (this file's trigger): trained cleanly, and
   *numerically* the line-integral recovery is much better than step 3 for
   most lines — but the actual spectra are still not good enough, and for
   Small specifically the shape is arguably worse-looking than before. See
   §1.

~50 MeV, out to 10^5-10^6 MeV) — a separate, smaller issue Part 3 wasn't
targeting.

**Small's real-vs-generated spectrum plot — the actual problem**: real
data is a clean, sharply single-peaked bump centered ~0.2-0.3 MeV, falling
off steeply on both sides, with narrow line spikes at ~1.5 keV
(Al K-alpha1), ~8 keV (Cu K-alpha1), and ~0.5 MeV (annihilation, right
at the peak's edge). The **generated continuum does not reproduce this

there), then only weakly and broadly overlaps the true peak region.
This is qualitatively different from — and arguably worse than — Part 2's
original failure mode (a spurious *hump*, but at least the true peak was
also visible): here the model just doesn't recover Small's peaked
unimodal shape, only a smoothed monotonic ramp.

## 2. Root-cause analysis

### 2a. The Cu/Ni-adjacent line over-generation (both sources)

CR's kept lines, in `log10(E/MeV)`: `Ni K-beta = -2.0828`,
`Cu K-beta = -2.0504` — **0.032 dex apart**. That is closer than
`continuum_repulsion_margin=0.05`, and almost certainly closer than
`build_gate_targets`'s Gaussian proximity bandwidth
(`match_tolerance_bins * local_bin_width`, evaluated at each line's own
position). Ni K-beta has 13,385 real events vs Cu K-beta's 1,915 (7x more
abundant) — if the two lines' proximity kernels in `build_gate_targets`
overlap meaningfully, a fraction of Ni's abundant real events get real
gate_target weight bled onto Cu's mixture slot, training the gate to
route to Cu's component far more often than Cu's true rate justifies.
Small's `Cu K-alpha1` sits similarly close to nothing else kept in this
run, but the same mechanism could apply against a *dropped* line (e.g.
`Cu K-beta`, count=32, dropped but still physically present in the real
spectrum at a nearby energy) contaminating `Cu K-alpha1`'s target — worth
checking directly.

**This is a real physical fact, not a stress-test artifact.** K-beta lines
of adjacent-Z elements are routinely this close in energy — our synthetic
stress test's 0.1-dex spacing was, in hindsight, still *more separated*
than this real case.

### 2b. Small's continuum shape collapse (the "worse than before" reaction)

CR (6 particle types, richer/broader continuum spanning many decades) came
out with a plausible continuum shape. Small (2 particle types, a single
sharp unimodal bump) did not. The common thread across both the Part 3
redesign's fixes (higher `beta`, continuum-repulsion) is that they all
*reduce the flexibility/expressiveness of the per-sample continuum
mean* — which is exactly the right lever to stop `mu_c(z)` from chasing
individual line-proximal training points, but it directly fights against
the continuum's ability to represent a **sharply peaked, low-entropy**
distribution like Small's. CR's richer continuum shape needs less precise
localization to look reasonable in a log-count plot; Small's needs *more*
precision (a single well-localized peak with true zeros on both sides), and
that's exactly the capacity we just took away.

**Conclusion: the fixed single-Gaussian-per-sample parametric form for the
continuum is the structural bottleneck.** No further tuning of
`beta`/`w_continuum_repulsion`/`continuum_repulsion_margin` on the *current*
architecture is likely to satisfy both CR's need for a wide multi-modal
shape and Small's need for a sharp, precisely-localized single peak at the
same time — turning the regularizer up helps one and hurts the other, which
is exactly the CR-vs-Small asymmetry observed. This matches Francesco's
read from the plots: the continuum is "too smoothed."

This was flagged as a known risk *before* Part 2 was ever built —
`docs/v0.8_IAF_integration_plan.md` §7 already recommends
`p(E|z,cond) = pi_cont * f_flow(E|z,cond) + sum_l pi_l * N(E; mu_l, sigma_l)`,
i.e. a **flow for the continuum term**, precisely because a single
parametric continuum component (Gaussian) can't be expected to capture
arbitrary real continuum shapes. We built the simplest version (single
Gaussian) first and are now hitting exactly the limitation that note
predicted.

## 3. Candidate directions for the continuum (pick one to prototype first)

All of these are strict continuum-side upgrades — the discrete
fixed-line-component half of the mixture (`pi_l`, `N(y; line_y[l], sigma_line)`)
stays as-is; only `pi_0 * N(y; mu_c, sigma_c)` gets replaced/enriched.

**(a) K-component continuum sub-mixture** (cheapest, smallest diff):
replace the single `(mu_c, sigma_c)` continuum component with a small
internal mixture of `K` (e.g. 3-5) Gaussians, each with its own learned
`(mu, sigma)` and a per-sample softmax over them — i.e. give the
continuum itself the same "mixture of Gaussians" structure the whole
energy head already has, one level down. Still closed-form NLL
(nested log-sum-exp), still trivial to sample from. Pros: ~30 lines of
code, reuses `gaussian_mixture_nll`'s pattern, fast to iterate. Cons:
still fundamentally a sum of smooth bumps — may still under-represent a
very sharp single peak with true hard zeros unless `sigma` is allowed to
shrink a lot (watch for the same z-leakage degeneracy re-appearing at the
sub-mixture level if `K` is too large relative to real structure).

**(b) Conditional Normalizing Flow for the continuum** (the
`docs/v0.8_IAF_integration_plan.md` §7 recommendation): a small
coupling/spline flow (RealNVP-style, since we need fast *sampling*, not
just fast density — see that doc's §3 note on IAF-for-posterior vs
coupling-for-prior/output, same logic applies here) mapping a 1-D base
noise variable to `y_cont`, conditioned on `(z, cond)`. Pros: essentially
unconstrained continuum shape, the "correct" fix per prior design work.
Cons: real new code (`core/flows.py` doesn't exist yet), a new dependency
(`tensorflow_probability`) if not already present, more moving parts to
get NLL + gradient checks right, harder to debug than (a).

**(c) Hybrid**: prototype (a) first on synthetic data that specifically
reproduces Small's pathology (single sharp peak + sparse tail + close
line pair), since it's cheap and may already be enough; only reach for (b)
if (a) provably can't represent a sharp single peak well even with several
components.

## 4. Fixing the line-collision problem (orthogonal to §3, do regardless)

Needs to happen no matter which continuum design wins:

- Cap `build_gate_targets`'s per-line Gaussian bandwidth at (some fraction
  of) the distance to the *nearest other candidate line*, not just
  `match_tolerance_bins * local_bin_width` — currently nothing stops two
  lines' bandwidths from overlapping when they're closer together than
  that width, which is exactly the Ni/Cu K-beta case.
- Alternative/complementary: when two candidate lines are closer than the
  detector's own resolution (or closer than the model can be expected to
  resolve), consider merging them into a single blended mixture component
  rather than pretending they're independently resolvable — needs a
  physically-justified threshold, not an arbitrary one.
- Re-check whether a *dropped* (count<100) line sitting close to a *kept*
  line is contaminating that kept line's `gate_target` (e.g. Small's
  dropped `Cu K-beta` vs kept `Cu K-alpha1` — confirm their actual energy
  separation before assuming this is or isn't in play).

## 5. Recommended sequencing for tomorrow

## 7. Implementation status (started same day, ahead of "tomorrow")

Work on §4/§5's sequencing started immediately rather than waiting:

**§4 (line-collision fix) - done.** `magi/data/preprocessing.py::build_gate_targets`
gained `max_bandwidth_frac_of_spacing=0.5` (default on): caps each line's
Gaussian bandwidth at half the distance to its nearest other matched line.
Verified directly against real CR data: at Ni/Cu K-beta's actual positions,
the uncapped bandwidth was 0.034 dex vs their 0.032 dex separation (i.e.
wider than the gap itself, confirming the diagnosis exactly); with the cap,
gate-target weight bleeding from Ni-proximal real events onto Cu's slot
dropped from 37.5% to 12.9% (Ni:Cu weight ratio went from 1.4:1 to 5.8:1).

**§3(c)/§5 step 2 (single-peak synthetic stress test) - done.** New script
`tools/synthetic_stress_test_small_singlepeak.py` (also see
`tools/synthetic_stress_test_cr_multimodal.py`, the earlier CR-oriented
version referenced in §0 step 4/§5 step 2): a single sharp
continuum peak (mimicking Small's real ~0.05-1 MeV bulk) + a sparse broad
low-energy tail + 2 lines 0.032 dex apart (mimicking Ni/Cu K-beta's real
separation) sitting inside that tail. Confirmed the test reproduces Small's
exact failure mode on the CURRENT (K=1) model, fully converged at 150
epochs: peak core under-filled (0.53x of real), both peak
shoulders/edges over-filled (6.7x, 7.2x), and a **330x** excess far above
the peak where real density is essentially zero - the single Gaussian gets
pulled wide trying to reconcile the sharp peak with the sparse tail, unable
to do either well. This is the smoking gun for §2b's diagnosis.

**§3(a) (K-component continuum sub-mixture) - implemented, mid-evaluation.**
`CVAE_MixEnergy_ContPhi_TaskAdaptive` gained `n_continuum_components=1`
(default preserves old behavior exactly - verified via a K=1 unit smoke
test). For K>1: `energy_gate_head`/`energy_cont_mu_head`/
`energy_cont_logsigma_head` widen from `1+n_lines`/`1`/`1` to
`K+n_lines`/`K`/`K` units; `_mixture_components` needed no change (already
generic); `_continuum_repulsion` now broadcasts over
`(batch, K, n_lines)`; the auxiliary gate cross-entropy pools the K
continuum slots' probability mass via log-sum-exp before comparing against
`build_gate_targets`'s `(continuum, line_1..line_L)` target (which has no
notion of which continuum sub-component an event belongs to - that split
stays unsupervised). `magi/validation/metrics.py::compute_line_integral_recovery`
gained `n_continuum_components=1` so `component_fraction` matches
`matched_lines[i]` against `energy_component_idx_gen == n_continuum_components + i`
instead of the old hardcoded `i+1`. Smoke-tested (K=3, gradients flow, loss
decreases, `generate()`'s component_idx correctly spans `0..K+L-1`).
**Evaluated against the single-peak stress test - K=3 (naive) made things
WORSE, not better:**

| metric | K=1 (converged) | K=3 (converged) |
|---|---|---|
| peak core (should be ~1x) | 0.53x under-filled | 0.39x under-filled (worse) |
| peak falling edge (should be ~1x) | 7.20x over-filled | 12.22x over-filled (worse) |
| far above peak (should be ~0) | 330x | 884x (worse) |
| line 0 / line 1 recovery ratio | 0.144 / 0.147 | 0.212 / 0.234 (lines slightly better) |
| line 0 / line 1 component_fraction (true=0.015 each) | 0.0059 / 0.0066 | **0.0048 / 0.0052** (worse) |

The K=3 run's generated continuum-component gate weights were
`[0.158, 0.831, 0.0004, 0.0048, 0.0052]` (5 slots = 3 continuum + 2 lines):
**one continuum sub-component absorbed 83% of all events, a second got
16%, the third collapsed to 0.04%.** This is a textbook Gaussian-mixture
component-collapse failure - nothing in the current design encourages the
K continuum sub-components to specialize into complementary shapes
(no diversity-promoting initialization, no entropy/balance regularizer on
the internal K-way split, and the auxiliary gate supervision only
constrains lines-vs-continuum-as-a-whole, saying nothing about how the
continuum's K slots should divide responsibility). Naively widening the
continuum from K=1 to K=3 just gave the optimizer more freedom to
mis-allocate capacity, without an explicit force helping it use that
freedom well - unbounded degrees of freedom made the optimization *harder*,
not the representation problem *solved*.

**Follow-up: collapse fix implemented and tested (K=3, same day).** Added
to `CVAE_MixEnergy_ContPhi_TaskAdaptive`: `w_continuum_balance=1.0` (a new
`_continuum_balance` regularizer - mean-squared deviation of the K
continuum slots' batch-mean usage, renormalized among themselves, from
uniform 1/K; a no-op when `n_continuum_components==1`) plus
`continuum_mu_init` (spreads the K sub-components' initial means, default
`linspace(-1, 1, K)` when not given, instead of all starting at 0 with no
symmetry-breaking pressure). Re-ran the identical K=3 single-peak stress
test with these fixes:

| metric | K=1 (baseline) | K=3 naive (collapsed) | K=3 + balance/init fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| continuum usage (3 slots) | n/a | **83% / 16% / 0.04%** (collapsed) | **34.6% / 37.9% / 26.7%** (balanced) |
| peak core (want ~1x) | 0.53x | 0.39x | 0.44x |
| peak falling edge (want ~1x) | 7.20x | 12.22x | 11.18x |
| far above peak (want ~0) | 330x | 884x | 357x |
| line 0/1 recovery ratio | 0.144 / 0.147 | 0.212 / 0.234 | 0.190 / 0.195 |
| line 0/1 component_fraction (true=0.015 each) | 0.0059 / 0.0066 | 0.0048 / 0.0052 | **0.0039 / 0.0043** (worst yet) |

**The collapse is genuinely fixed** - all three continuum slots now carry
comparable, non-degenerate usage, confirming the regularizer and spread
init work as intended. **But shape fidelity did not improve over K=1 once
collapse was fixed** - peak core, falling edge, and far-above-peak are all
still comparable to or worse than the plain single-Gaussian baseline, and
lines got squeezed even further below their true rate. This rules out
"collapse was the whole problem" - with balanced usage confirmed, the K=3
mixture still isn't decomposing the density into complementary
peak-vs-tail shapes; the most likely explanation is that nothing forces
the K components toward *different, complementary* shapes once collapse
is avoided - the spread init breaks initial symmetry, but training is free
to converge the K components back toward similar broad, mutually
redundant Gaussians (satisfying the balance constraint numerically without
actually specializing by local density), which fits the observed
"balanced but still smoothed/no-better" outcome.

**Conclusion: a discrete K-component Gaussian sub-mixture, even
non-collapsed, is not sufficient here.** The bottleneck isn't optimization
pathology (fixed) but representational: nothing about a handful of
independent Gaussians competing through a softmax gate naturally
decomposes a sharp-peak + sparse-tail shape the way it needs to. Per
§3(c)'s own escalation rule, this is the signal to move to §3(b) - the
conditional Normalizing Flow continuum - which models the density with a
single smooth invertible transform rather than discrete components
competing for responsibility, and so has no analogous
specialization/collapse failure mode to fight in the first place.
`n_continuum_components`/`w_continuum_balance`/`continuum_mu_init` stay in
the codebase (harmless at the K=1 default, and the balance-regularizer
machinery may still be useful if a flow-continuum design later wants a
small K-component sanity baseline to compare against) but are not the
path forward on their own.

## 8. §3(b) conditional Normalizing Flow continuum - IMPLEMENTED and passing the single-peak stress test

New code (all on branch `energy-mixture`, uncommitted working tree):
- `MAGI_package/magi/core/flows.py` (new): `ConditionalRQSFlow`, a 1-D
  conditional rational-quadratic-spline flow (Durkan et al. 2019) over
  `energy_y`. Not RealNVP coupling - coupling needs a vector to split, which
  a scalar target has none of; the 1-D analog with the same cheap-both-ways
  property is a monotonic spline whose knots come from a conditioner network.
  Backed by `tensorflow_probability==0.24.0` (new dep; added to
  `setup.py` + root `requirements.txt`; works alongside Keras 3 on the
  tensorflow-macos 2.16.2 / Metal stack with NO legacy-Keras flag - only
  `tfp.bijectors`/`tfp.distributions` are used, never tfp's Keras-dependent
  parts. `tf-keras==2.16.0` is pulled in transitively but not required at
  import). Spline domain `[-B, B]` (B=5) in a standardized space
  `y_std=(y-y_mean)/y_scale`; outside the domain tfp RQS is the identity, so
  an unbounded `N(0,I)` base is safe (verified) and the flow is a valid
  bijection on all of R with standard-normal tails.
- `MAGI_package/magi/core/losses.py`: added `gaussian_logpdf` (fully
  normalized, keeps the `log(2*pi)` constant) and `flow_line_mixture_nll`.
  The constant matters: the existing `gaussian_mixture_nll` drops it (valid
  only when every component is Gaussian and shares it), but the flow's
  `log_prob` is fully normalized, so the line components must be too or the
  log-sum-exp mixes inconsistent densities.
- `CVAE_MixEnergy_ContPhi_TaskAdaptive`: gated on a new
  `continuum_mode="gaussian"` (default, **byte-for-byte the old behavior -
  re-verified: the single-peak test at K=1 reproduces §7's baseline to the
  decimal, peak core 0.534 / falling edge 7.201 / far-above 330.5 / lines
  0.144,0.147**) vs `"flow"`. Also added `line_logsigma_trainable`
  (default True).

**Three findings while getting `continuum_mode="flow"` to actually converge
well (each fixed a distinct failure the previous one exposed):**

1. **Conditioning the continuum flow on `z` leaks and diverges at
   generation.** The first design (per the approved plan) conditioned the
   flow on `energy_cont_feat`, a function of the per-event latent `z`. At 5
   epochs it was excellent (peak core 1.00x), but by 150 epochs generation
   got *worse than the Gaussian baseline* (peak core 0.39x, far-above 697x)
   *even as val NLL kept improving* - the textbook aggregated-posterior /
   prior mismatch (`docs/v0.8_IAF_integration_plan.md` §2,§4): the flexible
   flow drives training NLL down by leaking per-event energy through `z`,
   but `generate()` draws `z~N(0,I)` and the marginal diverges. **Fix: the
   continuum flow conditions on `cond` (particle type), not `z`** - the
   continuum shape is a per-source marginal, and `cond` is supplied exactly
   at generation.
2. **The gate has the same `z`-leak.** With the flow on `cond` but the gate
   still on `z`, at 150 epochs the gate over-routed to the lines (generated
   mixing `[0.65,0.18,0.17]` vs true `[0.97,0.015,0.015]`, ~10-30x line
   over-generation), and *raising* `w_gate_aux` made it *worse* (it only
   sharpens the training-latent gate, worsening the prior-`z` aggregation).
   The Gaussian baseline hides this because its flexible per-`z` continuum
   absorbs the mismatch; the flow's fixed marginal exposes it. **Fix: the
   gate also conditions on `cond`, not `z`, in flow mode.** So the whole
   energy head p(E|cond) is a per-source spectrum (continuum shape + fixed
   lines at fixed weights), independent of `z`.
3. **Trainable line width lets lines eat the continuum.** Even with both on
   `cond`, a *trainable* `line_logsigma` widened to 3-9x the true resolution
   so the sharp line components could absorb sparse-tail continuum density -
   inflating their gate weight and depleting the peak. **Fix: pin the line
   width to the detector resolution** (`line_logsigma_trainable=False`,
   `line_logsigma_init=log(sigma_resolution)`). Then each line can only
   explain events at its own position and the flow must own the rest.

**Converged single-peak stress-test result (150 epochs, flow on `cond` +
gate on `cond` + pinned line width), vs the K=1 Gaussian baseline:**

| window (want ~1x, last row ~0) | K=1 Gaussian | flow (converged) |
|---|---|---|
| deep sparse tail | - | 1.05 |
| sparse tail | - | 1.05 |
| line cluster (both lines) | - | 1.37 |
| peak rising edge | - | 1.21 |
| peak core | **0.53** | **0.98** |
| peak falling edge | **7.20** | **0.85** |
| far above peak (real ~empty) | **330** | **1.00** (2 vs 2) |
| generated mixing weights | lines under-routed (0.006,0.007) | **[0.954, 0.011, 0.035]** (true [0.97,0.015,0.015]) |
| line 0/1 recovery ratio | 0.14 / 0.15 | **1.21 / 1.54** |

The sharp peak, both shoulders, the sparse tail, the empty high-E region,
and the line cluster are all reproduced within ~1.0-1.5x, and it is
**stable at convergence** (no degradation over training). The §2b/§7
continuum bottleneck is solved. Single seed (42); not yet multi-seed.

**Francesco's decision on the two design questions (2026-07-22):**

- **Energy<->geometry coupling MUST be preserved** - it is a fundamental
  feature of the data: a given energy (especially a material-dependent
  fluorescence line) is correlated with the volume/geometry it came from.
  That coupling is mediated by the shared latent `z`, so the energy head
  **must** condition on `z`. **Therefore fixes 1+2 (cond-only) are NOT the
  default** - they are demoted to an ablation. The code now exposes
  `energy_flow_condition="z_cond"` (**default**, preserves coupling: gate on
  `z` via `energy_feat`, flow on `z` via `energy_cont_feat`) vs `"cond"` (the
  ablation that passed this synthetic test). **Consequence:** the `z`-leak /
  aggregated-posterior mismatch is back in the default, so **faithful
  *generation* with coupling requires a learnable prior `p(z|cond)`**
  (IAF-plan Phase 2: a coupling/spline prior flow + MC-KL +
  `generate()` sampling `z ~ p(z|cond)` instead of `N(0,I)`). This is the
  real next step and is NOT yet built.
- **Line width: Francesco will provide the physical detector resolution**
  (in `log10(E)` space) to pin `line_logsigma` (`line_logsigma_trainable=
  False`, `line_logsigma_init=log(sigma_res)`) for the real run. Fix 3 stays.

**Important caveat about the acceptance gate:** the single-peak synthetic
test uses **noise geometry** (ur/uv/phi are pure noise, cond constant), so it
has NO real energy<->geometry correlation - a `z`-conditioned energy head can
only ever *leak* there, never exercise legitimate coupling. So this test
**cannot validate the physically-required `z_cond` default**; it validated
the flow's continuum *shape capacity* (via the `cond` ablation) and proved
the §2b/§7 representational bottleneck is solved by a flow. Validating
`z_cond` faithful generation needs either a new synthetic test WITH injected
energy<->geometry correlation, or the learnable prior + a real run.

**Not yet done / next steps (in order):**
1. **Learnable prior `p(z|cond)`** (IAF-plan Phase 2) - the blocker for

2. A synthetic test with real energy<->geometry correlation, to gate #1

## 9. Learnable prior `p(z|cond)` - IMPLEMENTED and passing the correlated A/B (2026-07-23)

Steps 1+2 above are done. See `docs/v0.8_learnable_prior_plan.md` for the
non-expert write-up of *why*; this section records *what shipped* and the

**What shipped** (branch `energy-mixture`, uncommitted working tree):
- **`core/priors.py::ConditionalCouplingPrior`** - a conditional affine
  (RealNVP-style) coupling flow over the 8-D `z`, conditioned on `cond`.
  Alternating masks, `tanh`-clamped log-scales, zero-initialized final Dense
  per layer so the prior *starts* at exactly `N(0,I)`. `log_prob(z,cond)` (for
  the KL) and `sample(cond)` (for generation), mirroring `core/flows.py`.
  Verified in isolation: exact round-trip, change-of-variables consistency to
  1e-5, and MC normalization `E[p/q]=0.977 +/- 0.030`.
- **`core/model.py`** gated on a new `prior="gaussian"|"coupling"` kwarg
  (default `gaussian` = byte-for-byte the old model). New `_kl_per` helper
  branches the KL: closed-form for gaussian, single-sample Monte-Carlo
  `log q(z|x) - log p(z|cond)` for coupling (reusing `gaussian_logpdf`).
  `generate()` samples `self.prior.sample(cond)` under coupling instead of
  `N(0,I)`. Prior weights train through the existing loss (no separate
  optimizer). Gaussian regression re-confirmed: the flow path reproduces §8's
  converged numbers exactly (peak core 0.98, far-above-peak 2/2).
- **`tools/synthetic_stress_test_coupled.py`** - the correlated gate the old

  appears ONLY in the low-`u_r` class). Real `corr(u_r,logE)=0.964`. Reports

## 10. Real-run prep - resolution pinning, checkpointing, line under-generation (2026-07-23)

Three real-run blockers were closed (Francesco chose X-IFU's 4 eV FWHM as the
detector resolution).

**(a) Per-line detector-resolution line widths.** `line_logsigma` is now a
per-line vector (was a single shared scalar; scalar init still broadcasts, so
backward-compatible). New helper `magi.line_logsigma_from_resolution(
line_energies_mev, resolution_ev, fwhm=True)`: a constant energy resolution DE is
a *linear*-energy width, but the head models lines in y=log10(E), so
`sigma_E = DE/2.35482` (FWHM) and `sigma_y = sigma_E/(E*ln10)` - which differs at
each line's energy. For X-IFU's 4 eV FWHM, sigma_y ~ 1e-4 (log-sigma ~ -9),
*below* the default `min_log_sigma=-6`; a *pinned* width now bypasses the
[min,max] clip (it can't drift anyway, and clipping made lines ~11x too wide),
while a *learned* width still clips. Pass the helper's output as
`line_logsigma_init` with `line_logsigma_trainable=False`.

**(b) Checkpoint save/load for the v0.8 class.** `training/checkpointing.py`
supported only v0.6/v0.7/v0.7.2. Added `model.to_generation_config()` (a 29-key
self-describing dict: line positions, continuum_mode, energy_flow_condition, flow
+ prior hyperparams, per-line-width trainability, etc.) and a loader branch in
`load_task_adaptive_model_for_generation` (encoder input is `[y_cont, cond]`, no
one-hot energy). Persist `to_generation_config()` as the model_config at save
time. Round-trip verified: pinned per-line widths and all decoder/flow/prior
outputs are bit-identical after save->load; generation finite.

**(c) Line under-generation on the dense cluster - FIXED via the gate-side
lever.** Root cause (confirmed with a flow-log-density-at-line-centers
diagnostic): the continuum gate weight is so dominant (pi_cont ~ 0.99 vs
pi_line ~ 0.003) that even the flow's modest density at a line center
out-explains the sharp line component, so the gate is never pushed to route line
events to line slots - a self-reinforcing under-routing. Two levers tested
(flow, z_cond, coupling, pinned, 150 epochs, seed 42):

| lever | line recovery (4 lines) | avg | mode-C core | between-line continuum |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| baseline (`w_gate_aux=0.3`) | 0.62/0.62/0.56/0.58 | 0.59 | 0.87 | 0.94/0.86 |
| flow-line repulsion `w=10` | 0.50/0.62/0.72/0.78 | 0.65 | 0.82 | **0.50/0.42 (holes)** |
| `w_gate_aux=1.0` | 0.97/0.70/0.78/0.72 | 0.79 | 0.77 | 0.80/0.94/0.93 |
| `w_gate_aux=3.0` | 0.89/0.71/0.75/1.00 | **0.83** | 0.90 | 0.65/0.57/0.86 |

- **The gate-side lever is the fix:** raising `w_gate_aux` (existing kwarg) lifts
  recovery from ~0.59 to ~0.8 while *preserving* the continuum. Recommend
  `w_gate_aux ~ 1-3` for sources with clustered lines (default 0.3 is tuned for
  the easy well-separated case, where lines already over-generate 1.2-1.5x).
- **Flow-line repulsion is a secondary, blunter knob** (new `w_flow_line_repulsion`
  kwarg + `_flow_line_repulsion`, default 0.0 = off; a `flow_line_repulsion`
  tracker; a no-op outside flow mode). It does suppress the flow at line centers,
  but for a *dense* cluster it evacuates the flow across the whole cluster band
  and digs holes in the legitimate between-line continuum. Leave off by default;
  only consider it at low weight to complement `w_gate_aux` if a specific line
  still leaks. `_continuum_repulsion` remains a no-op in flow mode.
